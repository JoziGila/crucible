# R7 physics review

Summary: (1) Every phase 1 `SignOff` stress and buckling check passes on an η99-calibrated `Estimated` bound whose coverage is marginal over FLX's corpus. Optimizers and agents select the designs where it undershoots (R1-04's failure mode, fixed only for surrogates), the miss rate never enters the budget's confidence, and classes have no computable membership (R7-01).
(2) The singular-point rule exists in three versions (contract, FLX, ING), and FLX flags the loaded patch that the canonical check never excludes, so the contract's canonical example can never pass (R7-02).
(3) "Distance to the nearest load" in L_local disables shells and beams under any distributed, gravity or inertial load, so R1-02's per-region fix fails for modal analysis, drop and pressure (R7-03). Close behind: phase 1 validates no physics model (R7-04), and eigenvalues have no error budget (R7-05).

### R7-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, COV, MEC, ING
**Section:** Contract 5 (`ErrorBound`, `ErrorBudget`), 7.1 Flux; FLX "Calibrated estimates" (D-FLX-24); COV "Error budgets and model form" (D-COV-17); MEC "Design checks" (D-MEC-30)
**Problem:** Every phase 1 `SignOff` stress and buckling check passes on a calibrated `Estimated` bound, and that bound carries only a marginal 99% guarantee over FLX's corpus. This is the failure mode R1-04 found for surrogates, and the contract fixed it only for surrogates: topology optimization and agents that minimize mass subject to a passing check select exactly the designs where the estimator undershoots, so coverage on optimized designs falls below the corpus rate. The calibration miss rate also never enters the budget's confidence: contract 5 union-bounds only "statistical, conformal, input distributions", so a `Pass` on an η99 bound reports no confidence at all. Finally, a class is a label the model author assigns (D-COV-17), with no computable test that a given result belongs to the population the 300 samples came from.
**Evidence:** FLX D-FLX-24: η99 is "the 99th-percentile underestimate of that estimator on its problem class". FLX-O-ERR-3: "Bound at least the true error in at least 99% of held-out cases per class". MEC D-MEC-30: "physics checks accept, at `SignOff`, calibrated `Estimated` bounds". Contract 7.1 applies the selection argument only to surrogates: "inside optimization loops calibration uses weighted conformal prediction". MEC's class for GP1 and GP2, "Linear elasticity, regularized peak, immersed octree", spans every geometry, cut fraction, Poisson's ratio and distance to a singular set.
**Fix:** (1) Contract 5: `Estimated { calibration }` carries its exceedance level α (0.01 for η99) and joins the union bound with the other confidence-carrying parts, so every `CheckOutcome` reports its total confidence. (2) Each calibration class in `std::sim::calibration` carries an applicability envelope over computable estimator features: degree p, minimum cut-cell volume fraction, cells per smoothing radius (ρ/h), distance to the nearest flagged singular set in units of ρ, Poisson's ratio and material contrast. A result outside its class envelope reports `Indicator`. (3) A `SignOff` `Pass` resting on an `Estimated` discretization part needs a refinement-consistency confirmation: one further level (h/2 or p + 1) whose value lies inside the first bound and whose own bound is smaller. Kiln's cost model counts the extra solve. (4) Every class corpus includes at least 20% adversarial samples, produced by `std::opt` runs that minimize the check margin inside the class envelope, and FLX-O-ERR-3 adds an adversarial search for undershoots, as FLX-O-ROM-2 does for surrogates. (5) Contract 7.1's sentence "Interactive bounds are reported already multiplied by their calibrated factor" becomes "every `Estimated` bound", matching D-FLX-24.

### R7-02 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, ING
**Section:** Contract 6 canonical example and 7.1 singular points; FLX "Regularized goals and singular points"; ING "Checks" outcome table
**Problem:** Three different singular-point rules exist, and under two of them the canonical check can never pass. The contract makes a point quantity `Undecided` "unless the check names a region excluding a stated distance from" every flagged set. ING makes it `Undecided` when "the region comes within the exclusion distance". FLX makes it `Undecided` when the quantity is within the exclusion distance. FLX's detector flags "edges of fixed or loaded patches", and the canonical check's region `p.away_from(clamp, 2 * thickness)` excludes the clamp but not the loaded `web.face(role: end_cap)`, so under the contract and ING the canonical example is always `Undecided { SingularPoint }`. ING's own `bracket_check` (`b.away_from(clamp, 8mm)` with a loaded `pad`) fails the same way. The detector is also blind to traction compatibility: a uniform normal traction over a whole face bounded by convex 90° edges is compatible at the corner and not singular, while a shear traction meeting a free face is.
**Evidence:** Contract 7.1: "edges of fixed or loaded patches) make point quantities `Undecided { SingularPoint }` unless the check names a region excluding a stated distance from them." Contract 6: `static_stress(p, fixed: clamp, force: load, on: web.face(role: end_cap))` and `max(over: p.away_from(clamp, 2 * thickness))`. ING table: "The region comes within the exclusion distance of a flagged singular edge | `Undecided { SingularPoint }`". At a 90° corner with traction t on face x and a free face y, equilibrium needs σ_xy = t_y on one face and σ_xy = 0 on the other, so only a shear component is incompatible.
**Fix:** State one rule in contract 7.1 and point to it from FLX and ING: (1) The detector flags a load or support patch boundary only where the prescribed condition is incompatible there: a patch boundary inside a smooth face, a corner where a shear traction meets a free face, or a clamped face meeting a free face. Re-entrant edges and material junctions stay flagged. (2) Every flagged set carries a default exclusion distance, max(2ρ, local wall thickness), which a check may override. (3) A regularized maximum is `Undecided { SingularPoint }` only when its near-maximal set (locations whose regularized value lies within the bound of the maximum) meets the exclusion zone of a flagged set; a region that merely contains a flagged set is not `Undecided`. (4) Rewrite ING's row accordingly. (5) Make the canonical example explicit about both ends: `max(over: p.away_from([clamp, web.face(role: end_cap)], 2 * thickness))`, and add an oracle that the canonical bracket decides at `SignOff`.

### R7-03 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX
**Section:** Contract 7.1 Flux (local slenderness criterion); FLX "Per-region models" (`flux::mark_reduction`)
**Problem:** The R1-02 fix does not hold for its main use. L_local includes "the distance to the nearest load", so any wall carrying a distributed load has L_local = 0 and t/L_local ≤ 0.2 never holds. Pressure on enclosures, tanks, hulls and wing skins is distributed, and so are gravity and inertia. Read literally, modal analysis (a shared phase 1 capability), GP5's drop test and every pressure load mark all thin walls `Solid`. That returns them to the volumetric octree that R1-02 showed needs 10⁶ to 10⁸ DOFs.
**Evidence:** Contract 7.1: "L_local is the smallest of the span between supports, the radius of curvature and the distance to the nearest load or feature". FLX repeats it: "computed from the study's conditions and Anvil's thickness, curvature and distance queries". FLX D-FLX-12 justifies reduction by golden products 2, 4, 5 and 8, all of which carry distributed or inertial loads.
**Fix:** Replace the definition in contract 7.1 and FLX with: "L_local is the smallest of the span between supports, the radius of curvature, the distance to the nearest concentrated condition (a point or line load, the edge of a load or support patch lying inside the wall, an opening or a feature), and, for distributed loads, the length |q|/|∇q| over which the load varies. Distributed loads, body forces and inertia that cover the wall do not shorten L_local." Add plates under uniform pressure, cylinders under internal pressure and a free plate in modal analysis to FLX-O-PLAN-2 as cases that must mark `Shell`.

### R7-04 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, COV, MEC
**Section:** Contract 13.1 phase 1 gate; COV "Evidence levels" (D-COV-3) and Open questions 3; COV Phasing phase 1
**Problem:** Phase 1 validates no physics model, so every phase 1 `SignOff` check is a conditional `Pass` whose release depends on a citable allowance catalogue that COV open question 3 leaves undecided. GP1 and GP2 measure geometry only, so no model reaches E3 under D-COV-3. All phase 1 physics stays E2 with model form `NotQuantified`, and contract 5 then needs a policy allowance "with a citation" for every check. GP2's gate also names no process, alloy or predicted quantity. The 13.4 rows that cite GP2 (strut buckling, inherent strain) therefore have no measured comparison that needs them.
**Evidence:** Contract 13.1: GP2 is "optimized and verified in Crucible; printed from Crucible's 3MF with the printer vendor's slicer; measured externally". COV phase 1: "E2 checks passing on cited allowances". COV open question 3: "Which design-code clauses state that their factors cover model uncertainty, per model class?" MFG's example build is `shop::pbf_ti64_400`, and MFG-O-AM-8 expects a "metal PBF build of GP2", but the contract never says so.
**Fix:** (1) Contract 13.1: GP1 and GP2 each add a proof-load test on three replicates, with strain gauges in each checked region and a load–displacement record, validated under V&V 10 with the V&V 20 metric. This raises immersed linear elasticity (GP1) and the beam-lattice model with localized strut stresses (GP2) to E3 in their Calibrated zones, so phase 1 releases need no allowance for those quantities. (2) Name GP2's process and alloy in 13.1, for example laser powder bed fusion of Ti-6Al-4V as MFG's example uses. (3) Answer COV open question 3: each std catalogue entry records code, edition, clause, factor, scope and whether the factor covers action-effect model uncertainty or resistance model uncertainty. Only action-effect entries cover the model form of a finite-element stress analysis. Each entry is confirmed by two non-author domain reviewers, as D-COV std review already requires. Until an entry is verified it cannot be cited.

### R7-05 · Major · Targets: FLX, MEC, COV
**Section:** FLX "Budgets and guarantees", "Eigenvalue problems"; MEC D-MEC-30; contract 13.4 row 1
**Problem:** Flux defines no error budget for eigenvalues, yet the first 13.4 row and MEC's buckling checks rest on them. The budget table covers energy bounds, linear goals and regularized peaks only, so a buckling load factor or a natural frequency has no estimator and can only be `Indicator`, which never decides `SignOff`. MEC names the class "Linear buckling, Lanczos or LOBPCG", which is a solver, not an estimator. Conforming Rayleigh–Ritz approximations of the lowest eigenvalue are upper bounds for the exact pre-stress, so the discretization error lies on the unsafe side of "load factor ≥ 2" and "first frequency above the excitation band". LOBPCG also needs a positive definite B matrix, which the geometric stiffness K_G is not.
**Evidence:** FLX budget table has no eigenvalue row; the only eigen error statement is "degenerate eigenspaces are reported as subspaces with Kato–Temple bounds", an algebraic statement. MEC D-MEC-30: "Buckling load factors | Calibrated `Estimated` | Linear buckling, Lanczos or LOBPCG". Goal-oriented eigenvalue estimation exists (Heuveline and Rannacher, Adv. Comput. Math. 15, 2001, https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Heuveline+Rannacher+posteriori+error+control+eigenvalue+problems&rows=3), and so do guaranteed lower bounds for Laplace-type operators (Carstensen and Gedicke, Math. Comp. 83, 2014, https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Guaranteed+lower+bounds+for+eigenvalues+Carstensen+Gedicke&rows=2).
**Fix:** Add an eigenvalue row to FLX's budget table. The discretization part is the DWR eigenvalue estimate, `Estimated { calibration }` in its own class and applied on the unsafe side. `Guaranteed` lower bounds apply only where a method proves them, such as Crouzeix–Raviart-type bounds for scalar diffusion on fitted simplices, never elasticity on cut cells. The algebraic part comes from Kato–Temple or Lehmann bounds, with the gap taken from the next computed Ritz value. Buckling solves K_G φ = μ K φ with K as the positive definite B matrix and λ = 1/μ_max. MEC renames the class "linear buckling factor, eigenvalue DWR, beam lattice" (and the octree variant), and FLX-O-SOLVE-6 adds an effectivity criterion on NAFEMS free vibration and Euler columns.

### R7-06 · Major · Targets: COV, FLX, MFG, CONTRACT
**Section:** COV "Dynamics and stability" (Buckling) and "Regime variables" (Imperfection sensitivity); COV "Solid mechanics" (feature ≥ 10 grains); MFG "Slicing, lattices, process simulation and DfAM" (as-built struts)
**Problem:** GP2's strut-buckling check uses linear elastic eigen-buckling, which overestimates the capacity of typical printed lattice struts. They are stocky enough to buckle inelastically, and their as-built waviness and diameter scatter reduce capacity further. COV's envelope refuses eigen-buckling only for shells flagged imperfection-sensitive, so struts pass unchecked. Flux has no operation that computes the Koiter coefficient the regime variable needs. The as-built strut diameters MFG calibrates arrive in phase 2, after GP2. COV's default "feature ≥ 10 grains" also puts many printed struts in the Extrapolated zone, and no strut-scale Material exists to fix that.
**Evidence:** The Johnson transition slenderness is π√(2E/σ_y). With typical Ti-6Al-4V values (E ≈ 110 GPa, σ_y ≈ 900 MPa, unverified handbook figures) it is about 49. A 0.6 mm strut 3 mm long has L/r = 3/0.15 = 20, well inside the inelastic range, where Euler's load exceeds the Johnson load. COV buckling envelope: "Eigen-buckling `Refuse` at `SignOff` on shells flagged imperfection-sensitive". MFG: "as-printed strut diameters calibrated per process", phase 2. FLX defines no Koiter computation.
**Fix:** (1) COV's linear eigen-buckling envelope adds the regime variable σ_cr,Euler / σ_proportional per member: Calibrated below 0.5, `Refuse` above 1. Escalation goes to the Johnson or tangent-modulus L0 rung for struts, or to nonlinear analysis with plasticity in phase 2. (2) It adds an imperfection regime variable for struts, the waviness amplitude over the radius of gyration from MFG's as-built calibration, `Flag` above a declared value with a Perry–Robertson-type reduction as the L0 alternative. (3) MFG's as-built strut calibration for metal PBF moves to phase 1 and enters 13.4, since GP2 needs it. (4) COV ships strut-scale Materials calibrated on as-built strut specimens, whose coverage includes strut diameter, so printed lattices are not Extrapolated by the grain default. (5) FLX adds `flux::koiter_coefficient` in phase 2 with nonlinear shells. In phase 1 the shell classifier is moot because shells start in phase 2.

### R7-07 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, MFG, COV
**Section:** Contract 13.4 row "Additive distortion by inherent strain"; FLX "Coupling" (element activation); MFG "Additive processes and build preparation", "Slicing, lattices, process simulation and DfAM"
**Problem:** Phase 1 predicts GP2's distortion with no supports, no baseplate removal and no stress relief, so the prediction cannot match what is printed and measured. Metal PBF supports are an MFG phase 2 capability. In phase 1 the vendor's slicer generates supports Crucible never sees, and the as-built shape the gate measures follows baseplate cutting, support removal and usually stress-relief heat treatment, none of which Flux models. GP2's gate compares no distortion prediction anyway (R7-04), so the 13.4 rule ("the first gate that needs it") does not put inherent strain in phase 1.
**Evidence:** MFG: "Supports | … block, line, cone and lattice supports (metal PBF) | … | MFG-O-AM-4 | 2". D-MFG-25: "In phase 1 … Crucible hands builds to the user's slicer". FLX: "Element activation switches cells on by a layer or time Field: per layer group for additive inherent strain (phase 1)". MFG-O-AM-8: "metal PBF build of GP2 within 0.1 mm in phase 2".
**Fix:** Move "Additive distortion by inherent strain" in 13.4 to phase 2, beside metal PBF supports and compensation, and update FLX, COV and MFG phasing. Specify the analysis as a declared sequence: layer-group activation of part plus supports (supports as a homogenized lattice material with calibrated stiffness), baseplate cut, support removal and optional stress relief (inherent strain scaled by a calibrated relaxation factor). Each step's data comes from MFG's coupons, for example cantilever coupons cut from the plate. If phase 1 keeps the row, the bureau's support geometry must be imported as an Asset into the build model; otherwise phase 1 states that distortion is not predicted.

### R7-08 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, MFG, ANV, ELX, COV
**Section:** Contract 13.4 rule and rows
**Problem:** The shared phase table phases each capability by the gate that names it, but not by what it depends on. Several prerequisites therefore land after the capabilities that need them: metal PBF supports (MFG 2) after inherent strain (1); as-built strut calibration (MFG 2) after GP2's strut checks (1); `anvil::boundary_layer` (ANV 4) after the phase 3 laminar conjugate heat transfer and 3D mould fill that FLX's own plan sends to fitted boundary layers; an eigenvalue estimator (none) under row 1; and a Koiter-coefficient computation (none) under COV's buckling envelope. The SPMe row omits FLX, although SPMe needs FLX's phase 4 parameter-domain spaces.
**Evidence:** ANV: "`anvil::boundary_layer` | … | ANV-O-BL-1 | 4". FLX plan defaults: "Contact, sliding interfaces, boundary layers; voxel data | Fitted meshes; finite cell". FLX: "Parameter-domain spaces | … | FLX-O-FORM-5 | 4" and "parameter-domain forms for SPMe cells" (phase 4), while 13.4's SPMe row lists "Also phased in: COV".
**Fix:** Add to the 13.4 rule: "A capability that a row or a gate depends on takes a phase no later than its dependent; each row names its prerequisites in a column 'Needs'." Then apply it: supports and inherent strain both in phase 2 (R7-07); as-built strut calibration in phase 1 (R7-06); laminar conjugate heat transfer and mould fill state an octree wall-resolution rule instead of fitted layers (R7-09); eigenvalue DWR in phase 1 under row 1 (R7-05); a Koiter computation in phase 2 under COV's nonlinear buckling; FLX added to the SPMe row's "Also phased in".

### R7-09 · Major · Targets: FLX, MFG, ANV, COV
**Section:** FLX "Solve plans and per-region discretization" (plan defaults), "Coupling" (conjugate heat transfer); MFG "Molds and injection molding" (D-MFG-15)
**Problem:** Phase 3 runs laminar conjugate heat transfer and 3D fill, pack and cool on the immersed octree, yet neither states how finely the wall-normal layers must be resolved, and FLX's plan default sends boundary layers to fitted meshes that Anvil delivers only in phase 4. The octree's only resolution rule, four cells across a section, comes from elasticity. It does not resolve a natural-convection thermal boundary layer or the frozen skin and gapwise shear profile that govern fill pressure, pack and warpage. GP5's measured warpage and hottest-part targets therefore have no defined resolution, and no estimator or GCI requirement says when the octree solution is converged.
**Evidence:** FLX: "Conjugate heat transfer | Partitioned with Robin conditions, or monolithic on a shared octree | 3 (laminar, natural convection), 4 (turbulent)". D-MFG-15: "a level-set fill front on the immersed octree for the Stokes-regime melt". ELX phase 3 evidence: "hottest part within ±5 K of measurement (targets)".
**Fix:** Amend FLX's plan default to "turbulent boundary layers needing y⁺ resolution: fitted layers from phase 4; laminar thermal layers and frozen layers: octree wall refinement". Add plan rules: at least five cells at p = 2 across δ_T = H·Ra_H^(−1/4) on heated walls, and at least eight cells across the cavity half-thickness for 3D fill and pack. Name both as regime variables in COV's convection and moulding envelopes, with under-resolution making the zone Extrapolated. Require solution verification (D-FLX-34) or an estimator for the fill-pressure and warpage goals at `SignOff`. MFG states GP5's expected 3D cell count as a target.

### R7-10 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, COV
**Section:** Contract 5 `ErrorBudget` composition and 13.1 validation metric; COV "Error budgets and model form" (D-COV-20)
**Problem:** Validation measures model-form error on particular system response quantities, but COV applies the result to every check the model serves, and the contract gives no rule for that transfer. Strain at three gauges or a tip deflection does not bound the model error of a regularized peak stress elsewhere. Under contract 5 the bounds sit in different norms (`Goal` of the validated quantity versus `Goal` of the checked functional), so a strict reading makes every E3 total `NotQuantified`, and a loose reading transfers the bound silently. V&V 20 also needs u_num as a standard uncertainty, while Flux reports bounds (`Guaranteed`, or `Estimated` at 99%), and no tab says how to convert one into the other.
**Evidence:** COV: "E3 or E4, Calibrated | `Estimated`: |E| + k·u_val at the nearest validation points". Contract 5: "bounds in different norms never combine (the total is `NotQuantified`)". Contract 13.1: "u_val = √(u_num² + u_input² + u_D²)".
**Fix:** Add to contract 5: "A model-form bound from validation applies to a check functional J only when the validation plan lists J's response-quantity class (quantity, location type and regime) among its validated classes; otherwise the model-form part is `NotQuantified { reason: UnvalidatedQuantity }`." Then (1) COV's `ValidityEnvelope.evidence` records the validated response-quantity classes; (2) D-COV-20 states the conversion u_num = B/√3 for a `Guaranteed` bound B (uniform) and u_num = B/z_{1−α/2} for an `Estimated` bound at level α; (3) contract 13.1 requires each physical gate's validation plan to place sensors on the checked quantities (R7-04).

### R7-11 · Major · Targets: FLX, COV, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-26; COV "Error budgets and model form"; contract 5 `ErrorSource`
**Problem:** Two producers fill one budget slot. `ErrorBudget.parts` is a map with one entry per `ErrorSource`, but D-FLX-26 files the shell and beam reduction error under "model form" while COV fills the same key from validation or `NotQuantified { NoValidationData }`. Which one wins decides whether every thin-wall check is a conditional `Pass`, and no rule says. The contract already has a `reduction or surrogate` source, which fits a reduction from 3D to a shell or beam. D-FLX-26 also leaves the 3D patch size unstated, though the patch must extend past the boundary layers that shell theory gets wrong.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "`parts: map ErrorSource → Component<Q>`" and its source list includes both "model form" and "reduction or surrogate". D-FLX-26: "Dimensional-reduction error is a model-form part". COV: "E2, any zone | `NotQuantified { reason: NoValidationData }`".
**Fix:** D-FLX-26 reports the dimensional-reduction estimate under `reduction or surrogate`, and COV's model-form part keeps its meaning for the 3D constitutive and physics model. Specify the patch: it extends max(3t, 2√(R·t)) beyond the goal region (R the local radius of curvature), and the estimate is `Estimated { calibration }` in its own class, which FLX-O-PLAN-4 calibrates.

### R7-12 · Major · Targets: PLT, FLX
**Section:** PLT D-PLT-35; FLX "Verification, validity and agreement" (FLX-O-ERR-10)
**Problem:** The R1-12 fix does not hold: requiring |q_A − q_B| ≤ E_A + E_B lets a class drift by the whole discretization error before anyone notices. Plans are hardware-independent, so when both classes solve the same discretization the discretization error is common to both and cancels in q_A − q_B. Only the algebraic, floating-point and step-control parts differ by class, and they are orders of magnitude smaller. A GPU pipeline that is wrong by 3% still "agrees" when the discretization part is 4%. The same rule fires false severity-1 incidents: with `Estimated` parts at 99%, about 2% of correct comparisons exceed the ratio 1, and a nightly corpus of thousands of quantities produces dozens of false incidents.
**Evidence:** D-PLT-35: "E is q's `ErrorBudget` total in q's norm plus an absolute floor"; "a ratio above 1 also fails the owning tab's effectivity oracle". FLX: "Plans depend only on their inputs, never on hardware". Contract 7.3: "A nightly divergence … opens a severity-1 determinism incident".
**Fix:** Rewrite D-PLT-35: when both classes ran the same resolved `SolvePlan` and the same adaptive marks, the tolerance is the sum of each class's algebraic, floating-point and temporal-control parts plus the absolute floor. Full budgets apply only when marks differ, and diverging marks are reported as their own finding. An incident opens when a `Guaranteed` or algebraic comparison fails once, or an `Estimated` comparison fails on two consecutive nightly runs.

### R7-13 · Major · Targets: FLX, COV
**Section:** FLX "Budgets and guarantees" (floating-point row), "Fidelities"; COV D-COV-23
**Problem:** `Interactive` GPU results have no valid floating-point or algebraic part. FLX's `Guaranteed` γₖ bound assumes the canonical reduction tree on the CPU. A WGSL kernel may flush subnormals, fuse and reorder, so γₖ does not hold for residuals or estimators computed on the GPU, and FLX does not say where the `Interactive` residual and estimator run. COV's precision class `F32Safe` already promises "f64 residuals evaluated canonically on the CPU", so FLX and COV disagree. Either the floating-point part becomes `NotQuantified`, and every GPU `Interactive` check is `Undecided`, or it is reported without a valid bound.
**Evidence:** FLX: "Floating point | A priori rounding bound γₖ of the canonical residual and estimator evaluation | … | `Guaranteed`"; "`Interactive` | … `Gpu(…)` in `f32` or `CpuIsa`". COV D-COV-23: "`F32Safe` (mixed precision with `f64` residuals evaluated canonically on the CPU)". The WebGPU conformance primer allows flush to zero: "an implementation may replace it with zero" (https://kdab.searchfox.org/firefox-main/source/dom/webgpu/tests/cts/checkout/docs/fp_primer.md).
**Fix:** FLX states: at `Interactive` on a GPU class, the solution Field is read back and its residual and estimator are evaluated on the CPU in `f64` with the canonical tree. The algebraic part covers solve and storage rounding, and the floating-point part is γₖ of that CPU evaluation. Estimators evaluated on the GPU are `Indicator` only. FLX's stage budget assigns this CPU evaluation to the 100 ms estimate stage, and η99 classes record whether they were calibrated on GPU-solved samples.

### R7-14 · Major · Targets: FLX, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX "Budgets and guarantees"; contract 5 (`ErrorBudget` kind rule)
**Problem:** The R1-01 fix is incomplete: no `Guaranteed` total can ever exist. R1-01 noted that an always-`Estimated` term makes `Guaranteed` totals unattainable, and the fix made rounding `Guaranteed` but left the algebraic part `Estimated`. Since a total's kind is its weakest part's, FLX's "Guaranteed energy bounds" (phase 2 and 3), "Guaranteed linear goals" and `Guaranteed` reaction resultants are all `Estimated` in total. Any physics check that declares `evidence: Guaranteed` cannot pass.
**Evidence:** FLX: "Algebraic | Equilibrated algebraic residual; solves stop below 10% of the discretization part | As discretization | `Estimated`". Contract 5: "its kind is the weakest part's". Papež, Rüde, Vohralík and Wohlmuth give "guaranteed, fully computable, and constant-free upper and lower a posteriori bounds on the algebraic" error over "a hierarchy of simplicial meshes" (CMAME 371, 2020; https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.cma.2020.113243?fields=title,abstract,year,authors).
**Fix:** On fitted simplicial meshes, where `Guaranteed` discretization bounds exist, the algebraic part uses the multilevel construction of Papež et al. on the multigrid hierarchy and is `Guaranteed`. After a direct factorization, the algebraic part is ‖r‖₂/√σ, where σ > 0 is certified as a lower bound on λ_min by the inertia of an LDLᵀ factorization of A − σM computed canonically. Everywhere else the algebraic part stays `Estimated`, and FLX's capability table states that the total is then `Estimated`.

### R7-15 · Major · Targets: FLX, MEC, COV
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-23 and Open questions 8
**Problem:** The default smoothing radius makes the checked quantity depend on part size, and for large parts it hides under-resolved peaks. At 1% of the region diameter, a 400 mm bracket (the canonical feature's maximum width) smooths with ρ = 4 mm across a 2 mm fillet. DWR then estimates the error of a quantity that never sees the fillet peak, while the discretization error of the peak goes unestimated; the regularization part compares only discrete Fields. At boundary points, where peaks sit, a volumetric Gaussian average over a half-ball adds a bias of first order in ρ·|∇σ|, not second order.
**Evidence:** FLX: "The default ρ is 1% of the region's diameter or a critical distance the check names"; "The regularization part is the gap to the enclosed raw maximum of the discrete Field". Contract 6: `width: Length in 20mm..=400mm`, `fillet(…, 2mm)`.
**Fix (answers FLX open question 8):** The default ρ is min(0.1 × the smallest boundary radius of curvature or fillet radius in R, 0.1 × local wall thickness), computed from Anvil's curvature and thickness queries and never from the region diameter. When the check names a fatigue or fracture criterion, ρ is the Material's critical distance for the theory-of-critical-distances point method. At boundary points the average is taken over a geodesic disc on the boundary surface, which removes the half-ball bias. ### R7-23 · Major · Targets: FLX, MEC
**Section:** FLX "Uncertainty quantification" (D-FLX-35, rare-event row, FLX-O-UQ-3)
**Problem:** The default worst-case tolerance propagation is not a worst case. Linearizing with adjoint gradients and checking with "one Hessian-vector product" probes curvature along one direction only. The second-order remainder over the tolerance box is unbounded, and where a response is stationary in some inputs (a symmetric gap, a peak at an optimum) the linear term is zero and the worst case is missed entirely. FLX does not say which bound kind the result carries. FORM is listed with an `Estimated` error part, though FORM has no error estimator and fails silently with several design points.
**Evidence:** D-FLX-35: "`std::uq::worst_case` linearizes the quantity with adjoint gradients, sums half-widths times absolute sensitivities over correlated affine noise symbols, and checks the linearization with one Hessian-vector product". FLX-O-UQ-3: "Bounds contain sampled extremes on mildly nonlinear cases". Rare-event row: "first-order reliability … `Estimated`".
**Fix:** `worst_case` runs k Lanczos steps on the Hessian over the scaled tolerance box (k Hessian-vector products, default k = 5), bounds the remainder by ½·|λ|max·‖h‖², and adds it to the bound. The result is `Estimated`, never `Guaranteed`, and `NonlinearResponse` fires when the remainder exceeds 20% of the linear term. FLX-O-UQ-3 defines "mildly nonlinear" by that ratio. FORM results are `Indicator` unless importance sampling around the design point confirms them, which gives an `Estimated` statistical part.
