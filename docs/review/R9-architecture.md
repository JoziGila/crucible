# R9 architecture and taste review

Summary:
1. Approvals do not bind what ships: the contract's `ApprovalSubject` covers only geometry and PMI and the release digest excludes the `ChangeId`, so firmware, material or document changes after approval keep the approval (R9-01).
2. Cached results still depend on things outside their keys: the Evaluation's fidelity ceiling (R9-02), evidence arrival time and the clock (R9-03), drag overrides (R9-07), shared crates under a frozen operation version (R9-06), and names or classes in two tabs' random streams (R9-09).
3. Core concepts have several owners or none (check identity, waivers, policy allowances, integrity endorsement, WIT packages, apps, limit failures: R9-04, R9-05, R9-14, R9-15, R9-25 to R9-27), and consumers phase capabilities before their producers (R9-31 to R9-33).

### R9-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, AGT, ELX, MFG
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`ApprovalSubject`); KLN "Revisions and lifecycle states" (D-KLN-26), "Change orders" (D-KLN-28); AGT "Acts and approvals" (D-AGT-20); ELX "Software and firmware as BOM items"
**Problem:** The binding `ApprovalSubject` covers only geometry and PMI, so an approved release does not bind the firmware, material, properties, documents or programs it ships. The release plan's digest deliberately excludes the `ChangeId`, so after approval anyone who can push may rebase onto a Change with the same subject but a different Software item binary or Material, and the approval stays valid. KLN's `ItemDefinition` is richer than the contract's subject but is defined only for Parts and Assemblies, and the contract wins where they differ.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "`ApprovalSubject` [KLN]: `{ base revisions, ItemDefinition GeometryIds with PMI attached by canonical entity number, check-verdict digest …, effectivity, evidence record ids }`". KLN l.490: "A revision's `ItemDefinition` holds the `GeometryId`s of the Part or Assembly with PMI …, the `Material` version, properties, the BOM by value and documents by semantic content" (no rule for Software, Document, `Program` or `Purchased` Items). AGT l.889: "A release plan's digest covers the `ApprovalSubject`'s `ObjectId`, the tag and the effectivity, never the `ChangeId`". ELX l.798: a Software item "records its item number, version, binary hashes, source repository and Change, toolchain". A firmware or material swap changes no `GeometryId` and no PMI, and changes a check-verdict entry only if some check happens to read it.
**Fix:** Contract 5 replaces the second element: "`ItemDefinition` digests of every affected Item (KLN), for every Item kind". KLN defines `ItemDefinition` per kind in one table: Part and Assembly (name-free identity per R9-12, PMI by canonical entity number, `Material` `ObjectId`, properties, BOM by value); Document (semantic content); Software item (binary hashes, software bill of materials digest, `load` bindings, toolchain digest); `Program` (posted text digest and its simulation evidence ids); `Purchased` (approved-manufacturer list entries). Late-bound fields stay placeholders. AGT's release plan digest keeps excluding the `ChangeId`, and `act.commit` refuses a tag target whose recomputed subject differs from the approved one. New oracle KLN-O-CHG-2: swapping a Software item binary, a Material version or a document after approval voids the approval, while a rename with `#[was]` does not.

### R9-02 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, KLN
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`Truth`); ING "`Approx<T>` and the decision rule" (D-ING-7); KLN "Progressive results"
**Problem:** Whether a branch on `Undecided` yields `Pending` or the `Failure` `ING6401` depends on the Evaluation's fidelity ceiling, which is in no key. Failures are cached values, and `Interactive` results are written to the shared cache, so a node computed under an `Interactive` ceiling stores `ING6401` under a key that a later `SignOff`-ceiling Evaluation (or another user) hits, and receives a failure instead of `Pending`. Results then depend on which Evaluation ran first, which breaks principle 4 and KLN-O-EQUIV-1.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "`if` on an `Undecided` fails loudly … at the Evaluation's fidelity ceiling; below the ceiling the node's `ResultStatus` is `Pending`". ING l.466: "At the ceiling it fails with `ING6401 UndecidedBranch`"; ING l.699: a `Failure` … "Kiln caches it like any value". KLN l.143: `Interactive` results are "Written" to the shared cache. Contract 7.2's six key items contain the effective fidelity but not the ceiling.
**Fix:** Make the node's value independent of the ceiling. A branch on `Undecided` at effective fidelity f always yields the cached value `Undecided { needed, fidelity: f }`, which propagates to dependents as `DependencyUndecided` and which `try` cannot catch. The ceiling affects only reporting: Kiln shows `ResultStatus::Pending` while a higher-fidelity node of the same target is demanded under the ceiling, and the Evaluation report raises `ING6401` when none is. Contract 5 `Truth` and `ResultStatus` adopt this wording; ING-O-35 adds "evaluate under an `Interactive` ceiling, then under a `SignOff` ceiling with a warm memo and shared cache; the second shows `Pending`, then the decided value".

### R9-03 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, MFG, ELX
**Section:** CONTRACT 2 (Evidence record); KLN "Physical tests and Evidence records" (D-KLN-35); MFG "Inspection and measurement"
**Problem:** Round 1's R3-17 found that a check's outcome for a fixed Change depended on when it was evaluated. The fix, "an evaluation reads the newest valid evidence record", keeps that dependence: two Evaluations of one Change and lockfile differ when a record arrives between them, two trusted builders can disagree, and incremental and cold builds can differ. "Valid" also reads the clock, since a record from an instrument "past calibration is invalid".
**Evidence:** Contract 2: "An evaluation reads the newest valid evidence record and stores its `ObjectId` in the `CheckOutcome`". KLN l.792: "A record from an instrument past calibration is invalid, and checks over it fail with `CalibrationExpired`". MFG l.563 repeats "Evaluation reads the newest valid record". KLN-O-EQUIV-1 requires incremental to equal from-scratch; contract 7.8 requires two builders to produce the same `ObjectId`.
**Fix:** Evidence selection becomes a function of a recorded input. Each Evaluation reads an evidence index at a transaction-log position, the evidence watermark, which is an input cell recorded in the Evaluation report and CI report. Protected branches advance the watermark by an audited ref transaction when records arrive, so a (Change, watermark) pair fixes every outcome, and two builders evaluate at the same watermark. Validity is judged at the measurement time (the instrument's calibration covered the measurement date), never at evaluation time. Contract 2 rewrites the sentence to "reads the newest record valid at its measurement time, up to the Evaluation's evidence watermark". KLN-O-HW-1 adds: two Evaluations at one watermark agree while a record arrives in between.

### R9-04 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, AGT
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`Labels`), 7.6 (context labels, free text); KLN "Labels and export control"; AGT "Context labels and write-down", "Instruction authority and untrusted content", "Risk classification", "Auto-merge" (D-AGT-16, D-AGT-17, D-AGT-24)
**Problem:** `integrity` is part of the label lattice that Kiln joins through every derivation, and catalog content, imported files and all third-party package data are untrusted origins. A real product contains catalog parts and imported STEP, so nearly every derived value carries `data_untrusted`, every agent context that reads one is flagged, every agent Proposal is at least R2, and auto-merge never applies. The contract says a human merge endorses typed values, but no rule says how endorsement changes the integrity label of the inputs Kiln joins, so either the flag saturates or it contradicts endorsement.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "The join takes the maximum level and the union of each set … Labels are joined through every derivation by Kiln". AGT l.750: "Untrusted origins include imported files, supplier, catalog and web content, all third-party package text". AGT l.653 (D-AGT-16): "any content from a `data_untrusted` context is at least R2"; D-AGT-17: auto-merge only for contexts "free of `instruction_tainted` and `data_untrusted`". Contract 7.6: "A human merge endorses structure and typed values, never free text".
**Fix:** Contract 5 states the integrity rule once: integrity attaches to input cells by provenance, and a human merge of the Change that introduced a typed input (a lockfile pin, an Asset, a source span) clears `data_untrusted` on that input's typed values; free text still needs per-span endorsement. Kiln joins integrity from input cells' current endorsement state, so values derived from a merged catalog pin are trusted data, while unmerged supplier submissions, extraction output and `read.value` handles stay `data_untrusted`. KLN-O-LABEL-1 adds two cases: a merged catalog pin yields trusted derived values; the same pin on an unmerged branch does not.

### R9-05 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, KLN, MEC
**Section:** ING "Checks" (`name` field, waivers); KLN "Proposals and merge" (`Conflict::Check { check: CheckId }`), "Diff, large assemblies and references"; CONTRACT 5 (`ApprovalSubject` check-verdict digest), 2 (Evidence record "addressed by the check it serves")
**Problem:** Check identity carries approvals, merge verdicts, metric diffs, evidence addressing and waivers, but nothing defines it. ING's default name comes "from the predicate's source", so the commonest edit, changing a limit from `0.6 * sy` to `0.65 * sy`, creates a new check: the merge rule then sees one check removed and another added rather than a verdict change, and Evidence records addressed to the old check stop applying silently. Two `require`s with equal predicate text collide, and `#[waive(check: "static_margin")]` names a check by bare name across the whole design.
**Evidence:** ING l.844: "`name` | From the predicate's source | Identity across edits; Kiln's diff and merge match checks by it". ING l.847: `x.require(…)` carries the check "into assemblies with the Instance path in its identity". KLN l.395 uses `CheckId`, which no tab defines; contract 5 lists "check id" in the approval subject.
**Fix:** Contract 5 defines `CheckId` = (the attachment's `OpSite` or Instance path, the `require` statement's text-derived identity under contract 5's statement rule, the `name` if written). The default never derives from predicate text, so editing the predicate keeps identity and the merge rule compares verdicts. Two checks with one identity in one attachment fail with a new `ING1103`. `#[waive]` takes the check's binding or full `CheckId`, never a bare name. ING-O-27 adds: editing a check's limit keeps its `CheckId` and its Evidence records.

### R9-06 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, FLX, ANV, PLT
**Section:** CONTRACT 4 ("frozen by content hash"); KLN "Scope and the operation contract" (D-KLN-2); ANV "Behavior versions" (D-ANV-18); FLX "Kernels, back ends and determinism"
**Problem:** Three mechanisms define what keeps an operation version's bits fixed, and the one Kiln enforces is the weakest. ANV freezes each version's source modules by hash; KLN accepts any code change whose outputs on a conformance corpus are unchanged; FLX only states the policy. A change in a shared result-affecting crate (`linalg`, the `kernels` back ends that compile std forms ahead of time, `simd`) that alters outputs outside the corpus keeps the key and silently poisons every cache that holds the old bits, across tenants.
**Evidence:** Contract 4: "Each operation version is frozen by content hash once released." ANV l.52 (D-ANV-18): "Each operation version, and every internal module it uses (for example `ssi@4`), is frozen by source hash". KLN l.37 (D-KLN-2): "Each operation version registers a digest of its outputs over a conformance corpus, and CI fails when code changes the digest without a version bump." FLX l.344: "Any output-bit change … is a new behavior version", with no freeze mechanism.
**Fix:** Contract 4 adopts ANV's rule for every operation: a released version freezes its source closure, meaning its own modules plus versioned modules of every result-affecting first-party crate it calls (`linalg@n`, `kernels` back end `@n`, `simd` horizontal ops `@n`), recorded as a closure hash in the operation registry. A change to a frozen module creates a new module version used only by new operation versions. KLN's registration refuses a released version whose closure hash changed; the corpus fingerprint remains a second check. Toolchain and `detmath` upgrades keep ANV's golden-hash rule, which correct rounding makes sound for `detmath` only. KLN-O-FPRINT-1 adds: a seeded change in `linalg` outside the corpus is refused by the closure hash.

### R9-07 · Major · Targets: ING, KLN, AGT, PLT, UIX, MEC, ELX
**Section:** ING "The Ingot IR and Kiln" item 5 (overrides); KLN "Graph model", "Keys"; AGT `evaluate.*` methods; PLT `PoolLane`; UIX D-UIX-15, D-UIX-27; MEC sketch drag; ELX router
**Problem:** The drag path, the most latency-critical path in the product, has no owner in Kiln or `api`. Literal overrides are input cells per ING, but KLN never mentions them, and a feature node's key (module code hashes, argument identities, `OpSite`, limits) does not include an override of a literal inside the feature's own body, so an overridden result can be stored and served under the un-overridden key. UIX, MEC and ELX route per-pointer tools through an "interactive edit lane" and an `api` edit stream that neither PLT's lanes nor AGT's catalog define.
**Evidence:** ING l.753: "A request may carry literal overrides keyed by `LiteralSite` … Kiln treats them as input cells". KLN l.54 lists input cells without overrides, and the word appears nowhere else in KLN. AGT l.312: `evaluate.build` takes "Targets, `Fidelity`, `estimate_only`". UIX l.593: tools "run in the engine process on an interactive edit lane and stream geometry each frame through `api`"; MEC l.180: "`api` Edit stream at 60 Hz"; PLT l.293: `pub enum PoolLane { Foreground, Background }`.
**Fix:** KLN adds overrides to its input cells and to rule 4 of "Keys": a node's key includes the canonical encoding of the overrides whose `LiteralSite` lies in its own code, and results computed under overrides are never written to the shared cache. AGT adds `evaluate.subscribe { targets, overrides, ceiling }` and an `edit.stream` method (a session-scoped stream of override updates ending in one `edit.apply`). PLT adds a third `PoolLane`, `Interactive`, preempting `Foreground` work at node boundaries, which KLN's priority table maps from the drag lane. ING-O-12 and KLN-O-PROG-1 add: a drag's overridden result never answers a request without the override.

### R9-08 · Major · Targets: KLN, ECO, PLT, CONTRACT
**Section:** ECO "First-party WIT packages" (`crucible:host`, `crucible:geom`, `crucible:field`); CONTRACT 4 and 7.4 (evaluation worlds); KLN "Labels and export control"
**Problem:** ECO gives evaluation-world components `crucible:host/cas`, `crucible:geom/measure` and `crucible:field/sample`, which reach the engine outside `crucible:ops`. A component that can read any CAS object by `ObjectId` can read data whose label never joined its node's label, so its outputs can carry content at a lower label than its sources; and measurements and samples taken outside `crucible:ops` are not recorded Op dependencies. ING states there is "no second path to the engine".
**Evidence:** ECO l.358: "`crucible:host` | `diagnostics`, `progress` (write-only), `cas`, `parallel` | | KLN"; ECO l.356: "`crucible:geom` | `types`, `build`, `measure`". ING l.946: "Operation calls go through `crucible:ops/anvil` and `crucible:ops/flux`, served by Kiln as recorded Op dependencies | No second path to the engine". Contract 5: labels "are joined through every derivation by Kiln".
**Fix:** `crucible:host/cas` reads only objects reachable from the call's input handles (capability-style handles, never raw `ObjectId`s), and every read is recorded as a dependency whose label joins the node's. Measurements and samples that compute are `crucible:ops` operations (`anvil::measure_*`, `flux::sample`); `crucible:geom` keeps types and builders only, as D-ECO-7 already says of it. `crucible:host/parallel` is specified by PLT with the determinism rule of its combinators. KLN-O-LABEL-1 adds: a component holding a hard-coded `ObjectId` of a confidential object cannot read it.

### R9-09 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, FLX, COV, ELX
**Section:** CONTRACT 7.1 (label invariance), 7.2 (last paragraph); KLN "Keys" rule 2 and `OpCx::rng`; FLX "General operations for non-variational methods"; COV "Platform notes" (Monte Carlo determinism); ELX "Analyses and results"
**Problem:** The keying of random streams is stated four ways, and two of them break other rules. Contract 7.2's "class-independent part of the key" still contains `ObjectId`s, which hash names, so it contradicts 7.1's label invariance; FLX keys streams "by the Op key", which includes the class, so CPU and GPU runs draw different samples (round 1's R3-11). Only KLN's form is consistent.
**Evidence:** Contract 7.2: "Random streams are keyed by the class-independent part of the key." Contract 7.1: "random seeds come from geometry, topology and argument values, never from names." FLX l.450: "counter-based random streams (Philox, keyed by the Op key)". COV l.782 and ELX l.523: "keyed by the class-independent part of the Op key". KLN l.74: "keyed without class, fidelity or names (by `GeometryId`s and argument values)".
**Fix:** Contract 7.2's sentence becomes KLN's: "Random streams are keyed by the operation identity, argument values and input `GeometryId`s or name-free content hashes, never by class, fidelity, `ObjectId`s of named values or `OpSite`s." KLN owns the mechanism (`OpCx::rng`); FLX, COV and ELX replace their sentences with one line pointing to KLN. ANV-O-LABEL-1 adds a Monte Carlo golden check under re-randomized names.

### R9-10 · Major · Targets: KLN, CONTRACT
**Section:** KLN "Live documents" (D-KLN-22) against "Labels and export control"; CONTRACT 7.1 (`repo`, live replication)
**Problem:** Live replication splits blocks by label, but KLN's labels attach to repositories, paths, Items, records and CAS objects, not to blocks, and nothing defines what an uncleared principal's local repository, compile or Git export holds for a file whose blocks carry a higher label. The desktop is local-first and authoritative for unpushed work, so an uncleared engineer must hold a `SourceFile` they may not read, or an undefined partial one, and cannot compile the module.
**Evidence:** KLN l.439: "blocks labelled above a document's label live in a separate document that only cleared participants receive". KLN l.673: labels "attach to repositories, paths, Items, records, CAS objects and API responses". KLN l.347: `SourceFile` is "Canonical Ingot text, a block map … and span integrity provenance", one object per file. D-KLN-33: "the local repository is authoritative for unpushed work".
**Fix:** The unit of a source label is a module (one file). A higher-labelled feature lives in its own module; its public signature may carry a lower, declassified label, so uncleared principals compile against the signature and evaluate it through derived views on cleared workers (D-KLN-31's remote evaluation). The live document unit becomes (package, module label set), which keeps contract 7.1's rule. KLN states this in Labels and export control, and KLN-O-LABEL-1 adds: an uncleared clone compiles and evaluates a design whose ITAR module it cannot read.

### R9-11 · Major · Targets: KLN, CONTRACT
**Section:** KLN "Changes and refs" (`Change` row); "Electronic signatures" (D-KLN-42)
**Problem:** The `Change` object includes its signature and `ChangeId` is the object's `ObjectId`, so the signature is part of the identity. D-KLN-42 requires dual signing by phase 2 and re-timestamping long-retention records before an algorithm retires. Adding an ML-DSA signature or a new timestamp to an existing Change would change its `ChangeId` and every descendant's parent pointer, rewriting history and voiding every tag, Proposal and signature that names it.
**Evidence:** KLN l.345: "`Change` | Parents, tree, author, … conflicts, signature; `ChangeId` is its `ObjectId`". D-KLN-42: "long-retention records are re-timestamped into an evidence chain before an algorithm retires"; contract 7.8: "Every signature record carries an algorithm id".
**Fix:** Signatures over Changes are detached signature records naming the `ChangeId`, which hashes the unsigned Change; a Change may gain signatures and timestamps without changing identity. The same rule holds for tags, release records, Evidence records and every signed CAS record: identity covers content, and signatures are records about it. KLN-O-SIG-2 adds: adding a second-algorithm signature to a year-old Change leaves every `ChangeId` and tag unchanged.

### R9-12 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, MEC, ANV
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 ("Two identities for every named value"); ANV "Two identities and two stages" (D-ANV-24); KLN "Revisions and lifecycle states"
**Problem:** The contract promises a `GeometryId` for every named value, but only Bodies have one. Revision identity, approval subjects and interchangeability use "`GeometryId`s of the Part or Assembly", and an Assembly's identity must drop Instance names and paths, order Instances without names and include placements and mates. Nobody defines that encoding, so renaming an Instance either changes an Assembly's revision definition or relies on an unwritten rule.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "Two identities for every named value: `GeometryId` hashes the canonical encoding with names, lineage and name-keyed attributes removed". ANV l.136 (D-ANV-24): "Every Body has a `GeometryId` beside its `ObjectId`". KLN l.490: "the `GeometryId`s of the Part or Assembly". MEC owns `Part`, `Assembly` and `Instance` (contract 5).
**Fix:** Contract 5 states the rule for each named type, and MEC owns the encodings: a Part's name-free identity hashes its Body's `GeometryId`, Material `ObjectId`, properties and PMI by canonical entity number, without name or item number; an Assembly's hashes the canonically ordered multiset of (child identity, placement, mates by canonical entity number) without Instance names or paths; Fields keep FLX's (`GeometryId` plus canonical numbers). ANV-O-LABEL-1 extends to Assembly identities under re-randomized Instance names.

### R9-13 · Major · Targets: COV, ING, FLX, KLN
**Section:** COV "Materials system", "Data model" (D-COV-13); ING "`Approx<T>` and the decision rule"; CONTRACT 6 (canonical example)
**Problem:** D-COV-13 makes a property read's value depend on the check that consumes it: the read resolves to the lower or upper basis by the sign of that check's sensitivity. Ingot is pure and strict, so `let sy = p.material.yield_strength(at: 20degC)` has one value before any check runs, and one binding feeding two checks of opposite sign cannot hold both. ING has no type or rule for this, and the canonical example's check depends on it. It also violates D-FLX-5 ("a consumer never changes its producer") and makes check evaluation need derivatives through every solve that reads a property.
**Evidence:** COV l.641: "Property reads resolve to a statistical basis on the side the check needs. Form-level derivatives give the sign of the check's sensitivity". ING contains no mention of basis or of sensitivity-resolved values. Contract 6: `p.require(stress < 0.6 * p.material.yield_strength(at: 20degC))`.
**Fix:** A property read returns `Approx<Q>` whose `input and tolerance` part is a `Guaranteed` enclosure from the lower to the upper basis value under the policy's basis (specification limit, else B-basis). The existing decision rule then decides conservatively with no consumer dependence: `x < 0.6 * sy` is `True` only if it holds at the lower basis. Where the property feeds a solve, Flux propagates the enclosure as an input part (by sensitivity sign at the frozen discretization, or by solving at both ends when the sign varies), which is D-COV-13's computation moved into the producer. Check reports name the basis and the side that decided. COV revises D-COV-13; ING adds one row to the decision-rule table; ING-O-8 adds a two-check case.

### R9-14 · Major · Targets: KLN, CONTRACT, ING, AGT
**Section:** KLN "Revisions and lifecycle states" (release guard); CONTRACT 7.6 ("Passing every check"); ING "Checks" (`severity` row)
**Problem:** Three texts state what blocks a release, and they disagree. KLN requires every check, of any severity, to hold a non-provisional `Pass` or a signed waiver, and says nothing of conditional passes. ING says only `Error` blocks; the contract's agent rule counts a conditional `Pass` only under a covering policy allowance. The policy allowance itself, the record that turns a conditional `Pass` into a gate pass, has no schema, signer or phase in KLN, which owns repository policy; it appears only in KLN's interface row to COV.
**Evidence:** KLN l.507: "Entering `Released` requires every check to hold a non-provisional `Pass` on a release-accepted class or a signed waiver". ING l.841: "`Error` blocks protected merges and releases; `Warning` and `Info` report". Contract 7.6: "every `Error`-severity check has the verdict `Pass` at its declared fidelity, a conditional `Pass` counting only under a covering policy allowance". KLN l.852 (to COV): "policy allowances and `min_evidence_level`"; neither term appears elsewhere in KLN. COV l.811 requires "policy allowances with citations".
**Fix:** Contract 5 defines one predicate, `gate_passes(outcome)`: severity `Error`, non-provisional, verdict `Pass` with every `conditional_on` component covered by a policy allowance, on a release-accepted class, or a Waiver (R9-15). KLN defines `PolicyAllowance { error_source, scope (check kinds, Items, model families), allowance as an `Estimated` component, citation (code and clause), signers }`, a policy-package record changed only through `propose.policy` with two signers, in phase 1 because GP1's checks are conditional on model form. Contract 7.6, KLN's release guard and merge queue, and ING's `severity` row cite the predicate instead of restating it. KLN-O-PLM-1 adds a `Warning`-only failure that must not block, and a conditional `Pass` without an allowance that must.

### R9-15 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, KLN, COV, MFG, ELX, AGT
**Section:** ING "Checks" (`#[waive]`); KLN release guard ("signed waiver"); COV "Policy by zone" (human waiver in the change order); MFG "Sustainability and compliance" ("a person records a time-bounded waiver"); ELX "Electrical rule checks"; KLN, MEC and ELX parts-policy waivers
**Problem:** "Waiver" is one concept with four mechanisms and no glossary entry: a source attribute any editor can write, a signed waiver at release, a human-only waiver in the change order for envelopes, and recorded waivers for parts policy. Which one a release accepts, who may author it, how it expires and what it names differ by tab, and agents can write the source form, which then shows as a waived status in ELX's requirement view.
**Evidence:** ING l.853: "A waiver is an attribute, `#[waive(check: "static_margin", reason: "…", until: "C")]`". KLN l.507: "or a signed waiver". COV l.168: "Human waiver with rationale; agents cannot waive". MFG l.651: "unless a person records a time-bounded waiver in the change order". ELX l.792: status "waived with a reason".
**Fix:** Contract 2 adds **Waiver**: a signature record by an authorized human naming a `CheckId` (R9-05), the Items and effectivity it covers, a reason and an expiry (revision, date or serial range), attached to a Proposal or change order. `#[waive]` in source is only a request that review shows; it counts nowhere until a matching Waiver record exists. Agents may write the attribute, never the record; `Protected` checks and `Refuse` envelopes accept none. ING, KLN, COV, MFG, ELX and MEC cite the entry instead of defining their own.

### R9-16 · Major · Targets: ING, MEC, KLN
**Section:** ING Open questions 3 ("Instances at geometry-driven positions"), "Assemblies, variants and families" (D-ING-22); MEC "Standard parts and fasteners" (`fasten`); KLN "Effectivity and variants" (D-KLN-27)
**Problem:** ING's existence rule forbids an Instance whose existence depends on a computed value, which rules out one fastener per hole of a resolved query. MEC's std `fasten(cover_holes, …)` does exactly that, so the two tabs contradict each other on the commonest assembly operation of GP3, GP4 and GP8. ING's proposed answer (a configuration count plus a check against the resolved holes) makes every added hole a two-place edit.
**Evidence:** ING l.1280: "Static Instance existence (D-ING-22) forbids a fastener per hole of a resolved query. Proposal: the count comes from a configuration parameter and a check compares it with the resolved holes". MEC l.513: `let joint = fasten(cover_holes, spec: FastenerSpec::iso4762(…))`, which "picks the shortest standard length" per hole. ING l.589: "Creating an Instance under any other condition, including a loop over a computed count, fails with `ING2601`".
**Fix:** Decide: separate a usage's existence from its multiplicity. An Instance group created by iterating a resolved query is one usage whose existence follows the static rule (configuration parameters and constants) and whose multiplicity and per-member parameters (length, placement) are computed. The 150% BOM keeps its SAT checks over usage existence and lists the group with a computed quantity per configuration point, which BOMs already express as a quantity line. A group may be empty only if its usage declares `min: 0`. ING revises D-ING-22 and `ING2601` to name this exception; MEC's `fasten` returns such a group; KLN-O-PLM-5 adds a bolt-circle fixture whose quantity follows a hole-count parameter.

### R9-17 · Major · Targets: ING, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** ING "Arithmetic on tolerances" (symbol keys, `.rss`, `.monte_carlo`); CONTRACT 7.1 (label invariance)
**Problem:** Tolerance noise symbols are keyed by site and by the realizing Part or Instance path, and the site is an `OpSite`, so both keys are names. ### R9-36 · Minor · Targets: ING, KLN
**Section:** ING Open questions 10 (R2-06 declined); ING "Call sites and names"

**Evidence:** ING l.512: "a same-name call renames only later ones of that name". ING l.1288: "inserting a same-name call renames later same-name calls in its statement".
