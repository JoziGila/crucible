# R6 feasibility review

Summary: Phases 0 and 1 as specified need about 465 engineer-years, and the contract has no schedule, staffing or critical path; at a realistic hiring ramp the phase 1 gate lands 5 to 6 years out, while the cuts in R6-02 give about 200 engineer-years and a gate near month 36.
Contract v3 holds no performance targets and no fixed reference laptop, and several budget rows are infeasible as worded: early-edit drag preview in 100 ms, 1M Instances at 60 fps on an integrated GPU, 60 fps orbit on HiDPI panels, 33 ms p99 input-to-photon, 80% of libCEED throughput without FMA.
Platform claims rest on runtimes that their own maintainers rate below Tier 1 on Crucible Tier 1 targets (Wasmtime: Windows ARM64 Tier 3, Apple silicon Tier 2), on WebGPU that is off by default on most Linux GPUs and on Windows ARM64, on an iPadOS plan that ignores guideline 4.7, and on browser memory that never shrinks.

### R6-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, PLT
**Section:** CONTRACT §13 Roadmap and golden products; Phasing in every tab
**Problem:** The contract fixes gates but no duration, headcount, budget or critical path, and the phase 1 gate is the union of twelve tabs' phase 1 lists. Summed bottom-up, phases 0 and 1 as specified need about 465 engineer-years (EY, ±40%). At a net ramp of 25 to 35 senior specialists a year (planning assumption for Rust geometry, numerics, GPU and compiler hires), the phase 1 gate lands 5 to 6 years after start, which defeats the purpose of a wedge.
**Evidence:** Estimate per tab, phases 0 and 1, as specified → after the cuts in R6-02: ANV 70 → 32 (certified SSI, cellular Booleans for all 16 representation pairs, blends, offsets, healing, implicit meshing, HLR); UIX 60 → 30 (own toolkit, code editor, IME, native and web accessibility, three renderer tiers); KLN 55 → 18 (Loro live documents, cloud SaaS on FoundationDB, Cedar, transparency logs, encryption domains); FLX 45 → 20; PLT 45 → 25 (detmath, four platform layers, eight package formats, TUF deltas, crash pipeline, CI matrix); ING 30 → 14; AGT 30 → 10; MFG 30 → 3; ECO 25 → 9; MEC 20 → 12; COV 10 → 6; ELX 5 → 1; oracles, corpora, QA, design, docs, security and SRE 40 → 20. Total about 465 → about 200. Comparables: Zed built its own GPU UI framework and editor and shipped Windows on 15 October 2025 with "a full-time Windows team" (zed.dev/blog/zed-for-windows-is-here); Fornjot, a code-first Rust B-rep kernel, was archived on 19 June 2026 because "Its goals were never reached" (github.com/hannobraun/fornjot); Bevy's open virtual geometry still named culling "the biggest bottleneck" with DAG quality unfinished (jms55.github.io, 27 March 2025).
**Fix:** Add contract §13.1 "Program plan", with PLT owning engineering capacity:
1. Phase 0: 12 months, ramp from 20 to 59 people, about 40 EY. Gate unchanged, evaluated on the corpus that exists.
2. Phase 1: 24 months, ramp from 59 to 100 people, about 160 EY. Scope per R6-02, gate wording per R6-05, staffing per R6-03, sequencing per R6-04; gate review at month 36.
3. Every later phase gets the same table (duration, headcount, EY, critical path), approved at the previous gate (R6-34).
4. Plans count agent swarms as at most a 1.5× productivity factor, applied only to oracle-covered, well-specified work (format codecs, function ports, widget sets, test corpora) and never to research items (SSI topology, blends, cut-cell quadrature, AMG quality, GPU driver defects).
5. A scope addition above 5 EY to an open phase needs the same approval as a gate change.

### R6-10 · Major · Targets: UIX, PLT
**Section:** UIX Renderer, Frame budgets; UIX Transparency; PLT Budgets (orbit row)
**Problem:** The frame budget is set at 1920×1080, but the macOS mid machine (a MacBook Pro) and many Windows laptops drive panels of about 4 to 8 MP at their default scale. Resolve, edges, ambient occlusion, transparency and anti-aliasing scale with pixel count, so the 6.0 ms of pixel-proportional GPU work grows to about 17 ms on a 5.9 MP panel (2.8× the pixels), and 60 fps fails before geometry cost is counted.
**Evidence:** UIX Frame budgets at 1920×1080: resolve and edges 2.5 ms; ambient occlusion, transparency and overlays 2.5 ms; tone map, filter and composite 1.0 ms. UIX cites XeGTAO at "about 2.39 ms on an Intel i7-1195G7 integrated GPU" at 1080p. UIX switches the k-buffer to k = 2 only "above 8 megapixels".
**Fix:** Add a render-scale policy to UIX: during camera motion the 3D view renders at most 2.1 MP and upsamples, while edges, overlays and text draw at native resolution; still frames accumulate at native resolution, which D-UIX-12 already does. State every frame-budget row per render megapixel, add "60 fps at native resolution" as a high-band row, and give X-ray views their own 30 fps row (R6-27).

### R6-13 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, PLT, ECO
**Section:** PLT Tablets and phones; PLT Open questions 1; CONTRACT §14 (No-JIT execution ruling); ECO The extension model, Running on every platform
**Problem:** The plan runs all Wasm, Ingot included, on Pulley on iPadOS and treats App Review as one open question. Pulley carries "a ~10x order-of-magnitude slowdown" and is "likely not suitable for compute-intensive tasks", and guideline 4.7 attaches obligations that no tab plans for to any software not embedded in the binary. A store-like package browser is itself unacceptable under 3.2.2(i).
**Evidence:** App Review Guidelines: 2.5.2 forbids downloading code "which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app"; 4.7 allows "HTML5 and JavaScript mini apps and mini games, streaming games, chatbots, and plug-ins" subject to 4.7.1 (privacy, filtering, reporting and blocking), 4.7.2 (no exposure of native APIs without permission), 4.7.4 ("an index of software and metadata … universal links") and 4.7.5 (age restriction); 3.2.2(i) rejects "an interface for displaying third-party apps, extensions, or plug-ins similar to the App Store"; 4.2.3(ii) requires disclosing first-launch downloads. `com.apple.security.cs.allow-jit` lists availability on macOS only (developer.apple.com). Pulley quotes from docs.wasmtime.dev/examples-pulley.
**Fix:** (1) Amend the no-JIT ruling: on iPadOS, Ingot and Wasm components run inside WebKit (a WKWebView worker, or the web build in a thin WKWebView shell), where JavaScriptCore runs WebAssembly in WebKit's own process as it does in Safari; Pulley stays the fallback. A phase 3 spike measures WKWebView memory limits, WebAssembly speed and WebGPU availability before the shell is fixed. (2) Ship std and first-party components inside the app binary. (3) Offer registry packages on iPadOS only as dependencies of an opened design, never as a browsable catalog, and meet 4.7.1, 4.7.4 and 4.7.5 (registry index with universal links, reporting, age ratings) and guideline 3.1 for paid packages. (4) Disclose first-launch downloads per 4.2.3(ii). (5) Keep remote evaluation as the path if review rejects downloaded components.

### R6-14 · Major · Targets: PLT, KLN, UIX
**Section:** PLT The browser target, Memory; KLN Memory management and eviction; UIX Very large assemblies (budgets) and Interaction budgets; PLT Budgets
**Problem:** WebAssembly linear memory never shrinks, so one large evaluation leaves the engine worker at its peak for the rest of the session, and the browser idle-memory row cannot hold. The tabs also disagree on the budget: PLT sets a 3 GiB residency budget, KLN a 2 GiB browser budget, and UIX adds up to 1.5 GiB of UI memory plus a 512 MiB GPU page pool, more than many tabs survive.
**Evidence:** WebAssembly memory-control overview: "WebAssembly memory can only grow and never shrink, and there are no APIs for releasing memory to the host"; the proposal "is in its early stages". PLT: "The residency manager's budget is 3 GiB on desktop browsers"; KLN: "2 GiB in browsers"; UIX: "UI memory, 1,000-part assembly ≤ 1.5 GiB host memory"; PLT Budgets browser column: "Idle memory … Under 300 MiB".
**Fix:** PLT owns one browser memory table, replacing KLN's and UIX's numbers. The engine worker keeps a fixed linear-memory cap (proposed 1.5 GiB). Evaluations whose estimated peak exceeds 512 MiB run in a disposable heavy worker with its own memory and compute workers, write results to the OPFS CAS, and terminate afterwards. Phase 0 measures the out-of-memory kill threshold of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on 8 GB and 32 GB machines, and sets UI, engine and heavy-worker caps below it; where the sum would exceed it, heavy evaluations go remote. The browser idle-memory row is measured after a heavy-worker recycle.

### R6-15 · Major · Targets: PLT, KLN
**Section:** PLT The browser target (Process and thread layout; Storage); KLN Deployment, Modes (browser metadata store)
**Problem:** Neither tab says what happens when a user opens Crucible in two tabs of one origin. The browser CAS, journal and metadata store depend on OPFS synchronous access handles, which take an exclusive lock per file, and on SQLite VFSs that refuse a second instance, so the second tab fails or corrupts state.
**Evidence:** MDN `createSyncAccessHandle`: creating a handle "takes an exclusive lock on the file", and a second `readwrite` handle throws `NoModificationAllowedError`. sqlite.org persistence: the `opfs` VFS needs `SharedArrayBuffer` and "no two database handles can have the same OPFS-hosted database open at one time"; `opfs-sahpool` fails when initialized "via two tabs to the same origin". KLN: "SQLite compiled to Wasm over the Origin Private File System".
**Fix:** Specify one engine per origin. The first tab's engine worker takes a Web Lock and owns every OPFS write handle (CAS packs, journal, SQLite on `opfs-sahpool`, which also works without cross-origin isolation). Other tabs run the UI module only and reach the owner's engine through a `MessagePort` brokered by a `SharedWorker`; when the owner closes, the next tab takes the lock and replays the journal. Add a two-tab oracle: edit in both tabs, close the owner, verify that no Change is lost.

### R6-16 · Major · Targets: PLT, KLN, FLX, UIX
**Section:** PLT The compute runtime, GPU compute and Process model (D-PLT-12); KLN Cancellation; FLX Execution, Placement and scheduling; UIX Field visualization (per-pixel evaluation)
**Problem:** Dispatch limits disagree (PLT 100 ms, KLN 50 ms, FLX 4 ms while the camera moves), and the two larger values stall the renderer, because the engine's compute device and the UI's render device share one GPU and the OS arbitrates between the two processes. wgpu's feature list offers external memory only for Vulkan, so on DX12 and Metal, the Windows and macOS defaults, every GPU-computed Field or preview that the UI paints round-trips through host memory and is uploaded twice.

### R6-17 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, PLT, FLX
**Section:** CONTRACT §7.3 Determinism (mechanisms); PLT Numerical determinism (D-PLT-18); FLX Kernel generation (D-FLX-8), State of the art (high-order operator evaluation), Open questions 1
**Problem:** Banning FMA outside `detmath` halves peak arithmetic throughput on every x86-64-v3, x86-64-v4 and ARM64 core for kernels built from multiply-add chains (sum factorization at p ≥ 3, dense factorization, NURBS evaluation, implicit tapes), to protect the two targets that lack FMA, x86-64-v2 and wasm32. (2) Phase 0 measures the no-FMA penalty for four kernel families (sum factorization at p = 2 to 6, dense LU, NURBS evaluation, implicit tapes) and publishes it in PLT's State of the art table. ### R6-37 · Major · Targets: PLT
**Section:** PLT Wasm component runtime, In the browser
**Problem:** PLT transpiles each component inside the engine worker with jco, while the contract says browsers run registry-built bundles and ECO rejects client-side transpiling. In-browser transpiling also adds a JavaScript toolchain to the browser payload and a second source of browser bundles to verify.
**Evidence:** PLT: "The engine worker transpiles each instrumented Wasm component once with jco's transpiler, run inside the engine worker"; CONTRACT §7.4: "browsers run registry-built bundles in their own engines"; ECO D-ECO-10 rejects "transpiling in the client".
**Fix:** PLT: browsers load registry-built bundles; components built locally and not yet published get their browser bundle from the desktop toolchain or the team server; remove jco from the browser engine.

### R6-38 · Major · Targets: PLT, UIX
**Section:** PLT Performance engineering, Budgets (cold start and installed size rows); UIX Platforms and devices, Browser specifics
**Problem:** The two tabs set different browser startup and payload targets for the same measurement.
**Evidence:** PLT: "under 2.5 s with warm HTTP cache; under 8 s on a first visit over 100 Mbit/s" and "Engine download under 25 MiB compressed per build"; UIX: "interactive in 3 s on a warm HTTP cache and 6 s cold on a 50 Mbit/s link, with a Wasm payload under 20 MB compressed".
**Fix:** PLT owns one row: warm start ≤ 2.5 s; first visit ≤ 8 s at 50 Mbit/s; initial Wasm (UI module plus engine) ≤ 25 MiB brotli-compressed, with translators, CAM, electronics and physics packages loaded on demand. UIX points to it.

### R6-39 · Minor · Targets: PLT
**Section:** PLT The platform layer, Library choices (D-PLT-4)
**Problem:** D-PLT-4 pins a 0.31 beta in phase 0 and moves to the stable release "once published", but 0.31 has been in beta since November with breaking changes in each beta and no announced date.
**Evidence:** winit releases: 0.31.0-beta.1 and beta.2 on 16 November, beta.3 on 4 September (many public enums made `#[non_exhaustive]`, a macOS window attribute removed); latest stable 0.30.13 on 2 March.
**Fix:** Pin a specific 0.31 beta commit on a Crucible-maintained patch branch with a named owner, budget half an engineer for upstream pen, IME and drag-out work, re-pin at each beta, and record the dependency in the register of R6-30.

### R6-40 · Minor · Targets: PLT
**Section:** PLT Packaging, distribution and updates (macOS entitlement paragraph)
**Problem:** PLT says the JIT entitlement "is exercised only when a Wasm component is installed or upgraded", but mapping precompiled code as executable at every load also needs the hardened-runtime exception, and Ingot modules compiled per edit, once the code generator exists, use it continuously.
**Evidence:** Apple: the entitlement governs whether "the app may create writable and executable memory using the MAP_JIT flag", and "The Hardened Runtime disallows this by default" (developer.apple.com, `com.apple.security.cs.allow-jit`).
**Fix:** State that the entitlement is in use whenever Wasmtime loads or compiles code, and base the macOS security review on dynamic code being present in every session.

### R6-41 · Minor · Targets: PLT, ECO
**Section:** PLT Tablets and phones (Wasm components row); ECO The extension model, Running on every platform (Android row)
**Problem:** Both tabs mark Google Play's policy on downloaded code as unverified. The policy permits code that runs in a virtual machine or interpreter with indirect API access, so Wasmtime on Android is allowed as long as components get no direct Android API access; separately, Wasmtime rates `aarch64-linux-android` Tier 3.
**Evidence:** Google Play Device and Network Abuse policy: "an app may not download executable code (such as dex, JAR, .so files) from a source other than Google Play. This restriction does not apply to code that runs in a virtual machine or an interpreter where either provides indirect access to Android APIs"; docs.wasmtime.dev/stability-tiers.
**Fix:** Replace the [unverified] marks with this citation and its condition (no world imports Android APIs directly), and add Android to the upstream Wasmtime item of R6-12.

### R6-42 · Minor · Targets: UIX, ANV
**Section:** UIX Renderer, Display of Implicit, SubD and Mesh Bodies; ANV Performance, parallelism and determinism, GPU use
**Problem:** UIX compiles each Anvil implicit expression to WGSL for direct rendering, so every edit of a lattice parameter creates a new pipeline whose shader compile lands inside the drag. Anvil already specifies a GPU tape interpreter with interval pruning that takes new tapes as data.
**Evidence:** UIX: "Direct implicit rendering compiles the Anvil expression to WGSL"; ANV GPU use table: "Implicit tape interpreter with interval pruning".
**Fix:** UIX renders implicits with Anvil's WGSL tape interpreter during edits, and compiles a specialized shader only for still views of an unchanged expression.

### R6-43 · Minor · Targets: UIX
**Section:** UIX UI toolkit, Text (D-UIX-4)
**Problem:** Grayscale antialiasing of unhinted 11 and 12 px text on 100%-scaled 1080p Windows and Linux displays looks soft beside native applications, and D-UIX-4 says nothing about hinting.
**Evidence:** UIX Typography sets tree rows and controls at 12 px and captions at 11 px; Skrifa's `outline` module covers "Loading, scaling and hinting of glyph outlines" (docs.rs skrifa 0.48.0).
**Fix:** Add to D-UIX-4: glyphs are hinted with Skrifa's hinter at UI scale below 1.5 on every platform, using the same code so layout and bitmaps stay identical across OSes, and unhinted above; the snapshot oracles run at both scales.

### R6-44 · Minor · Targets: KLN, PLT
**Section:** KLN The CAS, Hashing (D-KLN-14); PLT The browser target, Storage
**Problem:** SHA-256 compiled to wasm32 has no SHA instructions, so browser hashing runs several times slower than native, and the 1 GiB in 150 ms target has no browser counterpart. Browsers offer native SHA-256 in workers.
**Evidence:** MDN `SubtleCrypto.digest`: "available in Web Workers", supports SHA-256, "returns a Promise", and "does not support streaming input".
**Fix:** The browser CAS hashes each chunk (≤ 1 MiB, already independent under D-KLN-15) with `crypto.subtle.digest` from the engine worker and combines chunk digests through `Index` nodes; state a browser target (for example 1 GiB in ≤ 1 s with 8 workers), and keep the Wasm SHA-256 for small synchronous objects.

### R6-45 · Minor · Targets: PLT
**Section:** PLT The browser target, Cross-origin isolation
**Problem:** The isolated application must send `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`, which severs references to cross-origin popups, so popup-based OIDC or SAML sign-in and payment flows break; the section does not say how sign-in works.
**Evidence:** MDN COOP: with `same-origin`, cross-origin documents open in a new browsing context group, and "any references between the new document and its opener are severed"; `same-origin-allow-popups` exists for "a cross-origin service for OAuth or payments".
**Fix:** Specify redirect-based sign-in for the isolated application pages, or a separate non-isolated sign-in page that hands a token to the application by same-origin redirect, and add a sign-in test with the reference identity providers to the browser-isolation oracle.

### R6-46 · Minor · Targets: UIX
**Section:** UIX Renderer, Frame pipeline (G1 and G2), G2 paragraph
**Problem:** G2 draws visible clusters with one non-indexed `drawIndirect` that runs 384 vertex invocations per 128-triangle cluster and pads with degenerate triangles. Vertex work is therefore three invocations per triangle with no post-transform reuse, on the tier that has no compute rasterization to fall back on.
**Evidence:** UIX: "A vertex shader pulls cluster vertices from storage buffers and emits degenerate triangles past a cluster's triangle count."
**Fix:** The cluster-culling pass writes the surviving clusters' triangle indices into one compacted index buffer, drawn with a single `drawIndexedIndirect`, so shared vertices are reused and no degenerate triangles are issued; keep the per-cluster method as the fallback when the compacted buffer would exceed the binding limit.
