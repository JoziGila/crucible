## Second-review findings

### R8-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, ANV, MEC
**Section:** Contract 5 (`ApprovalSubject`, `GeometryId`); KLN Revisions and lifecycle states (D-KLN-26, D-KLN-28); ANV Attributes and certificates, Encoding and versioning; MEC Holes and threads (D-MEC-7), Weldments
**Problem:** Design attributes that define the product but not its nominal geometry live in Anvil's name-keyed side table, which `GeometryId` excludes, and no tab says they enter "PMI". A cosmetic thread changed from M5 to M5×0.5, a weld throat from 4 mm to 3 mm, a surface finish, a ply's material or orientation, or a `HoleSpec` tolerance class leaves every `GeometryId` unchanged. D-KLN-26 then reports the released Item as unmodified, and an Approval of the old subject stays valid, although the part is now a different part. Contract 2 says "Any change to the subject voids it", but the subject omits the change.
**Evidence:** ANV: "Design attributes (`HoleSpec`, `ThreadSpec`, `Weld`, finish, the `FeatureRole` role aliases of contract 5) live in a side table keyed by `EntityId`"; "only `ObjectId` sees names, lineage and name-keyed attributes". KLN: "A revision's `ItemDefinition` holds the `GeometryId`s of the Part or Assembly with PMI attached by canonical entity number, the `Material` version, properties, the BOM by value and documents"; D-KLN-26: "an Item is modified since release exactly when its `ItemDefinition` differs". MEC D-MEC-7: threads default to `Repr::Cosmetic`, "nominal geometry plus the record"; weld beads are `Cosmetic` by default and "bead geometry is never needed". No tab defines PMI to include `ThreadSpec`, `HoleSpec`, `Weld`, finish or `Ply`.
**Fix:** In contract 5, add: "Every attribute class declares `definitional: bool`. Definitional attributes (GD&T and size tolerances, datums, `HoleSpec`, `ThreadSpec`, `Weld`, surface texture and finish, `Ply` and laminate records, region material assignments, late-bound field placeholders excluded) are encoded name-free, keyed by canonical entity number, in a definition section whose digest enters the `ItemDefinition` and the `ApprovalSubject` beside the `GeometryId`s." Replace "ItemDefinition GeometryIds with PMI attached by canonical entity number" with "ItemDefinition GeometryIds with definitional attributes attached by canonical entity number". `FeatureRole` aliases and display styles are non-definitional. Add KLN-O-PLM oracle: changing one attribute of each definitional class on a released golden-product Item marks it modified and voids its pending Approvals; changing a non-definitional one does neither.

### R8-04 · Major · Targets: ANV, CONTRACT, ING, KLN
**Section:** ANV Fidelity (D-ANV-28), oracle ANV-O-FID-1; contract 7.1 Anvil; ING Fidelity, drags, holes and derivatives
**Problem:** At `Interactive` Anvil makes uncertified floating-point topology decisions, and ANV-O-FID-1 accepts that "differing `SignOff` decisions lie within ambiguity bands". A near-threshold decision that differs splits or merges faces, so `EntityId` discriminators, `ResolveError`s, query fixes accepted in the UI, drag-written seeds and agent Proposals built at `Interactive` can fail or resolve differently at `SignOff`. Nothing marks the `Interactive` result as resting on such a decision, and measurement bounds on it are reported as `Guaranteed`. Contract 7.1 allows only exact predicates and certified enclosures for decisions, with no fidelity exception.
**Evidence:** ANV: "`Interactive` | `Portable` floating-point topology decisions and sampled seeding, validated by `check_body`"; D-ANV-28 rejects "certified decisions at every fidelity (misses the rebuild targets)". Contract 7.1: "Exact adaptive predicates for linear decisions; certified enclosures with recorded, capped tolerance growth for decisions on curved geometry". ING: "A release demands every node at `SignOff`, geometry included".
**Fix:** Use filtered decisions at `Interactive`, as Shewchuk's adaptive predicates do: compute the distance in `f64` with a forward error bound e, decide directly when |q − τ| > e, and otherwise run the certified three-way rule. Topology then agrees across fidelities except where `SignOff` itself fails with `Ambiguous`. Keep sampled seeding and uncertified tracing at `Interactive`, since `check_body`'s global separation catches missed branches and forces recomputation. Change ANV-O-FID-1 to "`Interactive` and `SignOff` results have equal topology and equal `EntityId`s on the corpus". If the filter is rejected, the contract must state the exception and ING must make every query resolved on an `Interactive` Body `Pending` when a provisional decision touched its anchors.

### R8-13 · Major · Targets: KLN, ANV
**Section:** KLN Proposals and merge (condition 5, D-KLN-21); ANV HistoryRecord and lineage
**Problem:** Merge condition 5 flags a conflict when "both sides modify, split or delete the same base entity", but no tab defines what a side "modifies" relative to the base. If it means "same `EntityId`, different geometry", then a dimension change on one side (which moves every adjacent face) and a new hole on the other (which adds a loop to one of those faces) conflict, which is exactly the pair D-KLN-21 cites for rejecting region overlap. If it means `HistoryRecord`s, a parameter edit re-runs the operation and produces no `Modified` record against the base at all.
**Evidence:** KLN condition 5: "Both sides modify, split or delete the same base entity, or one side's new entity intersects the other side's modified region at or below the merge clearance". D-KLN-21: "*Rejected:* ... overlap of changed regions (flags the commonest pair of edits: a dimension change beside a new feature)". ANV `Modified { id, aspects }` already distinguishes geometry, boundary, tolerance, attributes and `Mirrored`.
**Fix:** Define each side's touched set as the base entities that carry a topological record (`Split`, `Merged`, `Deleted`, or `Modified` with aspect `Boundary` or `Attributes`) in an Op whose key differs between base and that side; geometry-only changes from parameter edits are excluded. A conflict is a nonempty intersection of the two touched sets, or a created entity on one side within the merge clearance (certified distance) of an entity in the other side's touched set. Add KLN oracles: width change plus a new hole on the same face merges clean; two fillets on one edge conflict; a hole and a pocket that both cut one face conflict only when they touch.

### R8-19 · Major · Targets: ANV, KLN, CONTRACT
**Section:** Contract 7.2 (two-stage keys, Failures); ANV Two identities and two stages, The operation contract (`OpError`)
**Problem:** The geometric stage is keyed by `GeometryId`s and shared across designs, and contract 7.2 caches Failures as values, but a geometric-stage failure cannot carry what `OpError` promises: `at` entities, `partial` Bodies with names, and `fixes` that are compiling source edits naming bindings and call sites. Notes (`ToleranceGrown`, `FragileAnchor`) have the same problem. Only geometric outputs get a relabeling stage, so implementers must either put names into the shared stage, defeating sharing, or drop the fixes.
**Evidence:** ANV: "The relabeling stage, keyed by the geometric output's id, the `OpSite` and the inputs' name maps, assigns `EntityId`s"; "`OpError { kind, why, fixes, at, partial }` is a deterministic `Failure`, cached and catchable". Contract 7.2: "A deterministic `Failure` ... is a value: catchable with `try`, cached".
**Fix:** The geometric stage returns `GeomFailure { kind, at: canonical numbers, partial: geometry-only Body, fix_templates }` and Notes over canonical numbers. A relabeling stage keyed by (failure or note digest, `OpSite`, input name maps) maps them to `EntityId`s and turns templates into `SourcePatch`es. State this in contract 7.2 ("both stages apply to failures and notes") and add a KLN-O-SITE case where two designs share one failing geometric stage and receive fixes that name their own bindings.

### R8-24 · Major · Targets: MEC, ING, KLN
**Section:** MEC Open questions 1; MEC Determinism and stability under edits (D-MEC-5); ING Builder blocks and sketches
**Problem:** The storage of solved seeds is open, yet it fixes the source format of every sketch and how sketch edits merge, both phase 1. A layout section merged by last writer would hold design-determining values outside Ingot text, which `crucible export git` ("writes Ingot text and `ingot.lock`") does not carry, and would silently drop one of two concurrent drags. Full-precision inline seeds rewrite many literals per drag and turn concurrent drags into textual conflicts.
**Evidence:** MEC open question 1: "Should seeds live in a layout section merged by last writer, or stay inline at rounded precision? ING and KLN must agree." MEC D-MEC-5: "the solved sketch is a deterministic function of seeds, constraints and the pinned operation version"; seeds also decide under-determined parts by minimum displacement.
**Fix:** Decide: seeds stay inline, rounded to a quantum q (default the larger of 1 µm and 2¹⁰ρ of the sketch, written as decimal millimetres). On release of a drag, write only entities whose solved position moved by more than q, and before writing re-solve from the rounded seeds and check that the branch qualifiers and the solution agree within the certified bound, else write more digits. Seed literals merge as ordinary literals; a conflict on both sides' seeds of one under-determined block is a real conflict, since those seeds are intent. Add MEC-O-SK-4 cases: a drag that moves nothing writes nothing; rounded seeds rebuild bit-identically.

### R8-26 · Minor · Targets: ANV, KLN
**Section:** ANV Encoding and versioning (D-ANV-9)
**Problem:** R2-17's fix holds only for defaults. "The hashed form omits optional values equal to their defaults", but a newer minor release that computes a non-default value for an optional column (a cached certificate, a new bound) on the output of an unchanged operation version gives that `OpKey` two `ObjectId`s across builders on different releases, which contract 7.8's two-builder agreement then rejects.
**Evidence:** ANV: "A minor version adds optional sections or columns, and the hashed form omits optional values equal to their defaults, so a minor release never changes an identity".
**Fix:** Add the rule: optional sections are either derived caches excluded from the hashed form entirely, or written only by operation versions released with or after that minor version. ANV-O-ENC-2 builds the corpus with the old and new minor and compares identities.

### R8-36 · Minor · Targets: KLN, ANV
**Section:** KLN Revisions and lifecycle states (D-KLN-26)
**Problem:** The default definition tolerance, the smaller of 0.1 µm and 1% of the tightest tolerance, is an absolute length below Anvil's resolution for Bodies whose diagonal exceeds 64 m (ρ = 2⁻²³ m ≈ 0.12 µm there), so any upgrade that moves a hull or building member by its own resolution marks the Item modified. "Keeps topology" also has no stated test.
**Evidence:** KLN: "moves geometry only within the definition tolerance (default the smaller of 0.1 µm and 1% of the Item's tightest tolerance)". ANV resolution table: a 300 m hull has ρ ≈ 0.48 µm.
**Fix:** Default to the larger of 4ρ and the smaller of 0.1 µm and 1% of the tightest tolerance, and flag designs whose tightest tolerance is below 4ρ. Define "keeps topology" as equal topology in Anvil's normal form with entities matched by `EntityId`, measured by `anvil::deviation`.

### R8-48 · Minor · Targets: ANV, PLT, KLN
**Section:** ANV Open questions 1 (D-ANV-18)
**Problem:** The CI cost of keeping every version executable is open.
**Evidence:** ANV open question 1: "PLT and Kiln must confirm the CI cost."

### R9-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, AGT, ELX, MFG
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`ApprovalSubject`); KLN "Revisions and lifecycle states" (D-KLN-26), "Change orders" (D-KLN-28); AGT "Acts and approvals" (D-AGT-20); ELX "Software and firmware as BOM items"

The release plan's digest deliberately excludes the `ChangeId`, so after approval anyone who can push may rebase onto a Change with the same subject but a different Software item binary or Material, and the approval stays valid. KLN's `ItemDefinition` is richer than the contract's subject but is defined only for Parts and Assemblies, and the contract wins where they differ.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "`ApprovalSubject` [KLN]: `{ base revisions, ItemDefinition GeometryIds with PMI attached by canonical entity number, check-verdict digest …, effectivity, evidence record ids }`". ### R9-02 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, KLN
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`Truth`); ING "`Approx<T>` and the decision rule" (D-ING-7); KLN "Progressive results"
**Problem:** Whether a branch on `Undecided` yields `Pending` or the `Failure` `ING6401` depends on the Evaluation's fidelity ceiling, which is in no key. Failures are cached values, and `Interactive` results are written to the shared cache, so a node computed under an `Interactive` ceiling stores `ING6401` under a key that a later `SignOff`-ceiling Evaluation (or another user) hits, and receives a failure instead of `Pending`. Results then depend on which Evaluation ran first, which breaks principle 4 and KLN-O-EQUIV-1.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "`if` on an `Undecided` fails loudly … at the Evaluation's fidelity ceiling; below the ceiling the node's `ResultStatus` is `Pending`". ING l.466: "At the ceiling it fails with `ING6401 UndecidedBranch`"; ING l.699: a `Failure` … "Kiln caches it like any value". KLN l.143: `Interactive` results are "Written" to the shared cache. Contract 7.2's six key items contain the effective fidelity but not the ceiling.
**Fix:** Make the node's value independent of the ceiling. A branch on `Undecided` at effective fidelity f always yields the cached value `Undecided { needed, fidelity: f }`, which propagates to dependents as `DependencyUndecided` and which `try` cannot catch. The ceiling affects only reporting: Kiln shows `ResultStatus::Pending` while a higher-fidelity node of the same target is demanded under the ceiling, and the Evaluation report raises `ING6401` when none is. Contract 5 `Truth` and `ResultStatus` adopt this wording; ING-O-35 adds "evaluate under an `Interactive` ceiling, then under a `SignOff` ceiling with a warm memo and shared cache; the second shows `Pending`, then the decided value".

### R9-03 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, MFG, ELX
**Section:** CONTRACT 2 (Evidence record); KLN "Physical tests and Evidence records" (D-KLN-35); MFG "Inspection and measurement"
**Problem:** Round 1's R3-17 found that a check's outcome for a fixed Change depended on when it was evaluated. The fix, "an evaluation reads the newest valid evidence record", keeps that dependence: two Evaluations of one Change and lockfile differ when a record arrives between them, two trusted builders can disagree, and incremental and cold builds can differ. "Valid" also reads the clock, since a record from an instrument "past calibration is invalid".
**Evidence:** Contract 2: "An evaluation reads the newest valid evidence record and stores its `ObjectId` in the `CheckOutcome`". KLN l.792: "A record from an instrument past calibration is invalid, and checks over it fail with `CalibrationExpired`". MFG l.563 repeats "Evaluation reads the newest valid record". KLN-O-EQUIV-1 requires incremental to equal from-scratch; contract 7.8 requires two builders to produce the same `ObjectId`.
**Fix:** Evidence selection becomes a function of a recorded input. Each Evaluation reads an evidence index at a transaction-log position, the evidence watermark, which is an input cell recorded in the Evaluation report and CI report. Protected branches advance the watermark by an audited ref transaction when records arrive, so a (Change, watermark) pair fixes every outcome, and two builders evaluate at the same watermark. Validity is judged at the measurement time (the instrument's calibration covered the measurement date), never at evaluation time. Contract 2 rewrites the sentence to "reads the newest record valid at its measurement time, up to the Evaluation's evidence watermark". KLN-O-HW-1 adds: two Evaluations at one watermark agree while a record arrives in between.

### R9-04 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, AGT
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`Labels`), 7.6 (context labels, free text); KLN "Labels and export control"; AGT "Context labels and write-down", "Instruction authority and untrusted content", "Risk classification", "Auto-merge" (D-AGT-16, D-AGT-17, D-AGT-24)
**Problem:** `integrity` is part of the label lattice that Kiln joins through every derivation, and catalog content, imported files and all third-party package data are untrusted origins. A real product contains catalog parts and imported STEP, so nearly every derived value carries `data_untrusted`, every agent context that reads one is flagged, every agent Proposal is at least R2, and auto-merge never applies. The contract says a human merge endorses typed values, but no rule says how endorsement changes the integrity label of the inputs Kiln joins, so either the flag saturates or it contradicts endorsement.
**Evidence:** Contract 5: "The join takes the maximum level and the union of each set … Labels are joined through every derivation by Kiln". AGT l.750: "Untrusted origins include imported files, supplier, catalog and web content, all third-party package text". AGT l.653 (D-AGT-16): "any content from a `data_untrusted` context is at least R2"; D-AGT-17: auto-merge only for contexts "free of `instruction_tainted` and `data_untrusted`". Contract 7.6: "A human merge endorses structure and typed values, never free text".
**Fix:** Contract 5 states the integrity rule once: integrity attaches to input cells by provenance, and a human merge of the Change that introduced a typed input (a lockfile pin, an Asset, a source span) clears `data_untrusted` on that input's typed values; free text still needs per-span endorsement. Kiln joins integrity from input cells' current endorsement state, so values derived from a merged catalog pin are trusted data, while unmerged supplier submissions, extraction output and `read.value` handles stay `data_untrusted`. KLN-O-LABEL-1 adds two cases: a merged catalog pin yields trusted derived values; the same pin on an unmerged branch does not.

### R9-05 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, KLN, MEC
**Section:** ING "Checks" (`name` field, waivers); KLN "Proposals and merge" (`Conflict::Check { check: CheckId }`), "Diff, large assemblies and references"; CONTRACT 5 (`ApprovalSubject` check-verdict digest), 2 (Evidence record "addressed by the check it serves")
**Problem:** Check identity carries approvals, merge verdicts, metric diffs, evidence addressing and waivers, but nothing defines it. ING's default name comes "from the predicate's source", so the commonest edit, changing a limit from `0.6 * sy` to `0.65 * sy`, creates a new check: the merge rule then sees one check removed and another added rather than a verdict change, and Evidence records addressed to the old check stop applying silently. Two `require`s with equal predicate text collide, and `#[waive(check: "static_margin")]` names a check by bare name across the whole design.
**Evidence:** ING l.844: "`name` | From the predicate's source | Identity across edits; Kiln's diff and merge match checks by it". ING l.847: `x.require(…)` carries the check "into assemblies with the Instance path in its identity". KLN l.395 uses `CheckId`, which no tab defines; contract 5 lists "check id" in the approval subject.
**Fix:** Contract 5 defines `CheckId` = (the attachment's `OpSite` or Instance path, the `require` statement's text-derived identity under contract 5's statement rule, the `name` if written). ### R9-06 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, FLX, ANV, PLT
**Section:** CONTRACT 4 ("frozen by content hash"); KLN "Scope and the operation contract" (D-KLN-2); ANV "Behavior versions" (D-ANV-18); FLX "Kernels, back ends and determinism"
**Problem:** Three mechanisms define what keeps an operation version's bits fixed, and the one Kiln enforces is the weakest. ANV freezes each version's source modules by hash; KLN accepts any code change whose outputs on a conformance corpus are unchanged; FLX only states the policy. A change in a shared result-affecting crate (`linalg`, the `kernels` back ends that compile std forms ahead of time, `simd`) that alters outputs outside the corpus keeps the key and silently poisons every cache that holds the old bits, across tenants.
**Evidence:** Contract 4: "Each operation version is frozen by content hash once released." ANV l.52 (D-ANV-18): "Each operation version, and every internal module it uses (for example `ssi@4`), is frozen by source hash". KLN l.37 (D-KLN-2): "Each operation version registers a digest of its outputs over a conformance corpus, and CI fails when code changes the digest without a version bump." FLX l.344: "Any output-bit change … is a new behavior version", with no freeze mechanism.
**Fix:** Contract 4 adopts ANV's rule for every operation: a released version freezes its source closure, meaning its own modules plus versioned modules of every result-affecting first-party crate it calls (`linalg@n`, `kernels` back end `@n`, `simd` horizontal ops `@n`), recorded as a closure hash in the operation registry. A change to a frozen module creates a new module version used only by new operation versions. KLN's registration refuses a released version whose closure hash changed; the corpus fingerprint remains a second check. Toolchain and `detmath` upgrades keep ANV's golden-hash rule, which correct rounding makes sound for `detmath` only. KLN-O-FPRINT-1 adds: a seeded change in `linalg` outside the corpus is refused by the closure hash.

### R9-07 · Major · Targets: ING, KLN, AGT, PLT, UIX, MEC, ELX
**Section:** ING "The Ingot IR and Kiln" item 5 (overrides); KLN "Graph model", "Keys"; AGT `evaluate.*` methods; PLT `PoolLane`; UIX D-UIX-15, D-UIX-27; MEC sketch drag; ELX router
**Problem:** The drag path, the most latency-critical path in the product, has no owner in Kiln or `api`. Literal overrides are input cells per ING, but KLN never mentions them, and a feature node's key (module code hashes, argument identities, `OpSite`, limits) does not include an override of a literal inside the feature's own body, so an overridden result can be stored and served under the un-overridden key. ING-O-12 and KLN-O-PROG-1 add: a drag's overridden result never answers a request without the override.

### R9-13 · Major · Targets: COV, ING, FLX, KLN

### R11-25 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, PLT, ECO
**Section:** Contract 7.8; KLN "Electronic signatures" (D-KLN-42); PLT "Signed updates" (D-PLT-37); ECO "Publishing and signing"

**Evidence:** PLT: WebAssembly memory "can only grow and never shrink"; D-PLT-30: "every evaluation estimated above 512 MiB runs in a disposable heavy worker"; the memory table rows "Engine worker | 1.5 GiB linear memory", "Idle, UI plus engine | 300 MiB after a heavy-worker recycle", "GP1 open | 1.5 GiB of linear memory in total". ### R12-19 · Major · Targets: PLT, KLN, FLX
**Section:** PLT The browser target (D-PLT-29, D-PLT-30)
**Problem:** D-PLT-29 makes the engine worker the owner of "every OPFS write handle (CAS packs, journal, ...)", while D-PLT-30's heavy worker "writes results to the OPFS CAS". Synchronous access handles lock per file, so the heavy worker cannot write those packs. The heavy worker also has no thread pool, because the wasm-bindgen-rayon compute workers share the engine module's memory, not the heavy worker's. Evaluations above 512 MiB, which include most `Interactive` and `SignOff` solves in browsers, therefore run single-threaded, or their pool start-up is uncounted.
**Evidence:** D-PLT-29: "The first tab's engine worker takes a Web Lock and owns every OPFS write handle"; PLT: a synchronous access handle "takes an exclusive lock on the file". D-PLT-30: the heavy worker "writes results to the OPFS CAS and terminates". The context table lists "Compute workers ... The compute pool through wasm-bindgen-rayon" beside "Heavy worker | One disposable evaluation with its own memory".
**Fix:**
1. The heavy worker writes a new pack file under a name only it holds, then sends the pack's index to the engine worker, which adopts it. D-PLT-29 then reads "every OPFS write handle except heavy-worker packs in transfer".
2. The heavy worker starts its own pool of min(`hardwareConcurrency` − 2, 8) nested workers on its own shared memory, the engine pauses its pool while a heavy evaluation runs, and Kiln's cost model includes pool start-up.
3. PLT-O-WEB-3 adds a heavy evaluation at 1 and 8 threads with identical hashes and the expected speed-up.

### R12-20 · Major · Targets: KLN, PLT
**Section:** KLN Deployment ("Team server | One container ... microVM CI"); PLT Tier 3 and servers; D-KLN-9
**Problem:** The phase 1 team server is "one container" that runs Proposal CI in Firecracker- or gVisor-class microVMs. Firecracker needs `/dev/kvm`, which ordinary cloud VMs and unprivileged containers do not expose, so the single-node server's sandbox works only on bare metal or with KVM passed through. The same host also runs the only trusted builder, and no tab states what separates the two pools on one machine.
**Evidence:** KLN: "Team server | One container: repository server, CI coordinator, microVM CI, optional workers". KLN: "In phase 1 the single-node team server is the only trusted builder ... and Proposal CI runs in microVMs on its host." Firecracker: "Firecracker requires read/write access to `/dev/kvm` exposed by the KVM module"; "EC2 only supports KVM on `.metal` instance types" (https://github.com/firecracker-microvm/firecracker/blob/main/docs/getting-started.md).
**Fix:** In KLN and PLT, the single-node team server becomes a host or VM install, not one container. It ships two untrusted-evaluation back ends: Firecracker where `/dev/kvm` exists, and gVisor otherwise. The installer reports which one is active, and the health check refuses Proposal CI when neither works. The trusted builder runs under a separate OS user with no access to the sandbox service's sockets or images. PLT-O-TGT-2 runs on a KVM host and on a cloud VM without nested virtualization.
