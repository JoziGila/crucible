### R1-01 · Critical · Targets: FLX, COV, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX "The error budget", "Operations on Fields" (Extremes), Rust `ErrorBudget::total`; contract §5 `ErrorBound` and `ErrorBudget`.
**Problem:** Flux's only `Guaranteed` discretization bounds (equilibrated flux and stress reconstruction) are energy-norm bounds, but checks test pointwise or extremal quantities (peak von Mises stress, junction temperature, gap under load), which an energy-norm bound cannot bound. The draft nonetheless adds "the discretization estimate" to a Bernstein-guaranteed extremum, and `total(norm)` combines parts given in different norms. The floating-point part is always `Estimated` and quadrature is `Estimated` for any non-polynomial integrand, so under the draft's own "weakest of the parts" rule no total can ever be `Guaranteed`.
**Evidence:** FLX: extremes are "Guaranteed for polynomial Fields via Bernstein bounds; plus discretization error"; budget table: "Floating point | Rounding, mixed precision, GPU `f32` | Bound from the final `f64` residual | `Estimated`"; `total`: "Combined bound in `norm`; the guarantee is the weakest of the parts." Reasoning: in 3D, H¹ does not embed in L∞ and stresses are only in L², so no energy estimate bounds a point stress or a maximum without regularity constants that are unknown for real geometry.
**Fix:** (1) Keep one `ErrorBound` per norm; `total(norm)` returns `Unknown` unless every part is already in that norm, and never converts. (2) Allow `Guaranteed` goal bounds only for bounded linear functionals (regional stress averages over a stated volume, reaction resultants, compliance, mean temperature), computed from the primal–dual product bound |J(u) − J(u_h)| ≤ ‖u − u_h‖_E · ‖z − z_h‖_E with equilibrated estimators for both factors plus algebraic and oscillation terms. (3) Make peak and pointwise checks `Estimated`, using DWR on a regularized maximum (a p-norm, or a Gaussian-weighted local average whose radius is part of the check), and document this in `std::sim::quick::peak_stress`. (4) Make the rounding term guaranteed by evaluating the `f64` residual with a rigorous a priori rounding bound (γ_n from the fixed reduction tree). (5) Add to contract §5: "A `Guaranteed` bound on a check's quantity requires a proof in that quantity's norm; energy-norm bounds never certify pointwise or extremal quantities."

### R1-02 · Critical · Targets: FLX, COV, ANV, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-12, D-FLX-13, "Immersed methods on octrees", plan table; contract §7.1 (Flux default discretization).
**Problem:** The default immersed volumetric octree does not fit the thin walls, lattices and frames of golden products 2, 4, 5 and 8: at two cells across, an inclined wall's shifted-boundary surrogate domain breaks into disconnected cubes, most cells in a thin wall are cut, and DOF counts exceed the 200,000-DOF interactive budget by one to three orders of magnitude. The escape hatch, IGA shells for walls thinner than 5% of the bounding size, uses a global criterion and leaves ribs, bosses and junctions unhandled. Flux has no beam or frame discretization, so welded frames, steel platforms and strut lattices have no efficient model.
**Evidence:** FLX: "The octree refines until every thin section has at least two cells across its thickness"; "Cut cells are a lower-dimensional fraction of all cells, so they do not dominate cost"; plan row "thin walls with thickness below 5% of the bounding size". For a wall with normal (1,1,1)/√3, a cube of side h spans √3·h through the wall, so a 2h-thick wall contains it only if its centre lies within about 0.13h of the mid-surface. A 200 × 150 × 50 mm enclosure with 2 mm walls holds 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ m³ of material: 1.5 × 10⁶ cells and about 3.6 × 10⁷ DOFs for p = 2 elasticity at 4 cells across, and 4.6 × 10⁶ DOFs at 2 cells across. A 100 × 60 × 40 mm bracket of 5 mm BCC cells with 0.8 mm struts needs about 8 × 10⁶ cells (2 × 10⁸ DOFs) at 4 cells per strut diameter. MEC "Weldments and structural frames" builds swept HEA members; no Flux table lists beam elements, and COV mentions "shell and beam FEM" only for phase 7 civil structures.
**Fix:** (1) Add dimensionally reduced discretizations in Flux phases 1–2: Timoshenko and Vlasov (warping) beams for members and struts, Reissner–Mindlin shells on Anvil mid-surfaces for walls, and rigid and Nitsche-based solid–shell–beam couplings as mixed-dimensional `dI` interfaces. (2) Replace the 5% rule with a local criterion: t/L_local ≤ 0.05 for Kirchhoff–Love and ≤ 0.2 for Reissner–Mindlin, where L_local is the smallest of the span between supports, the radius of curvature and the distance to the nearest load or feature; regions that fail stay solid. (3) Require at least 4 cells across any section for the shifted boundary method and check surrogate-domain connectivity against the Body's topology, falling back to aggregated cut cells with a typed note on mismatch. (4) Use beam-lattice models with node corrections for full-resolution lattice analysis and keep homogenization for design (R1-26). (5) Amend contract §7.1 to "immersed octree by default for compact solids; dimensionally reduced models for thin walls, members and struts, chosen by a stated local criterion."

### R1-03 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, COV, FLX, ING
**Section:** Contract §5 `ErrorBudget` ("A not-quantified component is never treated as zero"); COV D-COV-4 and "Error budget"; COV open question 3.
**Problem:** The contract and COV give incompatible semantics for unquantified components. Under the contract a check cannot pass while model-form error is unquantified, which COV says holds for every E2 model, so no std sign-off check could ever pass. COV instead evaluates on the quantified part and attaches an `unquantified` tag, which treats the missing term as zero for the `Pass` decision and violates the contract and principle 5.
**Evidence:** Contract §5 sentence quoted above. COV: "A check evaluates on the quantified part of its budget and carries an `unquantified` tag naming the missing components. Release policy in `repo` decides whether a tag is acceptable." COV OQ3: "E2 models have no measured model-form error."
**Fix:** Make the outcome explicit and typed: `Pass { conditional_on: [Component] }`, distinct from an unconditional `Pass`. A conditional pass meets a release gate only if repository policy names, for each unquantified component, a covering allowance with a citation (for example a design-code partial factor that the code states covers model uncertainty, applied to the demand). The check is then re-evaluated with that allowance included as a declared `Estimated` component; without such a policy entry the outcome is `Undecided { reason: Unquantified(components) }`. Write this rule into contract §5 and COV D-COV-4; ING's `CheckOutcome` carries `conditional_on`.

### R1-04 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, COV
**Section:** Contract §7.1 ("Surrogates count as Fields only with split-conformal bounds, an envelope test and a physics residual check"); FLX D-FLX-14; COV D-COV-7.
**Problem:** Split-conformal intervals give marginal coverage averaged over the calibration distribution, not a bound for the design being checked. An optimizer or agent searching design space steers queries to where the surrogate errs, breaking exchangeability even inside the training hull, so the envelope test does not catch it. The residual check is not a bound either: equilibrated flux reconstruction needs Galerkin orthogonality on vertex patches, which a surrogate prediction lacks. A surrogate-backed `Pass` therefore overstates certainty exactly where surrogates are used most.
**Evidence:** Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani, https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04684: "Existing methods such as conformal prediction offer marginal coverage guarantees"; "exact conditional inference guarantees are known to be impossible without imposing assumptions on the underlying distribution." Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, Ramdas, https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019: conformal prediction must be weighted by the likelihood ratio to go "beyond the case of exchangeable data." FLX D-FLX-14 says the residual check "bounds the error with the same equilibrated estimator used for solver results"; without orthogonality the local Neumann problems of the equilibration have incompatible data.
**Fix:** (1) Contract wording: "A surrogate prediction is a Field with kind `Estimated { confidence, scope: Marginal }`. It may drive Preview and Interactive results and design exploration; a SignOff check may use it only with a per-prediction a posteriori bound or a confirming solve." (2) Per-prediction bound: project the prediction onto the plan's finite element space, compute the total residual, and bound the error with an equilibrated estimator that includes the non-orthogonal residual through a global coarse solve, the same algebraic-error treatment Flux plans for inexact solves. (3) Inside optimization loops, calibrate with weighted conformal prediction using an estimated density ratio and locally adaptive (normalized or quantile-regression) scores, and report the effective calibration size. (4) Oracle: an adversarial optimizer run against each surrogate must not produce a sign-off `Pass` that the full solve fails.

### R3-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, ANV, KLN, FLX
**Section:** CONTRACT 7.2 (two-stage caching); ANV "EntityId", "Persistence and versioning"; KLN "Keys and behavior versions", "Revisions and lifecycle states" (D-KLN-26); FLX "Fields and errors (`flux-field`)"

### R5-03 · Critical · Targets: KLN, CONTRACT, AGT, FLX, ELX, COV
**Section:** KLN Distributed and remote evaluation (D-KLN-9), Threat model (Cache poisoning); contract §7.1 (Kiln)
**Problem:** The machines allowed to sign shared-cache attestations (CI runners and organization workers) also evaluate untrusted Proposals and run untrusted native code as External Ops (supplier FMUs, IBIS-AMI DLLs, emulators) inside ordinary Linux containers. One escape yields a signing identity that can map any `OpKey` to any output, and 1% spot re-execution catches a poisoned entry that a release consumes once with probability 0.01. Every tenant shares the global public cache domain, and no trust rule stops one tenant's builders from poisoning std results for all of them.
**Evidence:** D-KLN-9: "Only trusted builders write the shared result cache: CI runners and the organization's workers." KLN: "Kiln continuously re-executes 1% of shared cache hits"; residual risk "A compromised CI worker, caught statistically by spot re-execution". COV: "External tools run only in Linux containers on team servers or cloud workers (D-COV-12)". FLX: "FMUs from outside Crucible are untrusted native code." KLN: values with all-public inputs "live in one global public domain that every tenant shares".
**Fix:** (1) Split the worker pools. `untrusted-eval` workers run Proposal CI from agent and external branches and every External Op, in single-use microVMs (Firecracker- or gVisor-class). They hold no attestation key and write only to a per-Proposal namespace. `trusted` builders evaluate only merged protected-branch revisions. (2) A separate attestation service holds the signing keys and signs `(OpKey, ObjectId)` only for jobs dispatched to trusted builders, bound to workload identity. (3) Release gates and every `SignOff` Check accept a cached result only when two independent trusted builders produced the same `ObjectId` (different hosts; for `Portable` operations, different ISAs); otherwise they recompute. (4) Only the operator's own fleet writes the global public domain, with 2-of-n agreement. The default client trust policy is "own organization plus operator public signers". (5) Weight spot re-execution by downstream fan-out, at 100% for nodes that feed released Checks. (6) Hardware runners sign measurements with TPM-held device keys under measured boot. Reword contract §7.1 to: "Only trusted builders evaluating trusted inputs write the shared cache."

### R1-05 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, AGT, ING
**Section:** Contract §6 canonical example; FLX "Honest reporting of singular quantities"; AGT bracket example (09-agents line 587).
**Problem:** The canonical example clamps a whole face and checks `peak_stress`; the edges where the clamped face meets free faces carry an elastic edge singularity, so the peak diverges under refinement and, by Flux's own rule, the check is always `Undecided`. Flux's geometric detector lists only re-entrant edges, point measures and bi-material corners, and misses changes of boundary-condition type, contact edges and edges of loaded or fixed patches. The AGT example reports an impossible result: "worst margin `peak_stress` 31% (interactive fidelity, error estimate 4%)".
**Evidence:** Contract §6: `let stress = peak_stress(p, load, fixed: base.face(role: start_cap))`. FLX: "geometrically, by edges with dihedral angle above 180° and point measures". Reasoning: a 90° wedge with one clamped and one traction-free face has a Williams eigenvalue below 1 for ν near 0.3, so stress grows like r^(λ−1) toward the edge; the same holds along the edge in 3D.
**Fix:** Rewrite the example to support through the bolt-hole faces with a bearing or frictional condition and to check stress on a named region, for example `peak_stress(p, load, fixed: ..., region: solid.faces(role: fillet))`. Extend the detector to every edge where the boundary-condition type or the material changes, every contact boundary and every edge of a fixed or loaded patch; on such edges `peak_stress` returns `Undecided { SingularPoint }` unless the check names a region that excludes a stated distance from them. Correct the AGT example to match.

### R1-06 · Major · Targets: FLX
**Section:** FLX "The error budget" ("Flux uses equilibrated stress reconstruction for linear elasticity [unverified: guaranteed-bound theory for each element family]").
**Problem:** The constant-free Prager–Synge argument needs a reconstructed stress that is both equilibrated and symmetric, but practical reconstructions built from Raviart–Thomas rows impose symmetry only weakly, and controlling the antisymmetric remainder needs a Korn-type constant that is unknown for general domains. Curved boundaries and non-polynomial tractions add oscillation terms, and robustness as ν → 0.5 needs a mixed reconstruction. Labelling elasticity bounds `Guaranteed` would be false for the practical element families.
**Evidence:** FLX sentence quoted above; FLX's cited proof covers only "reaction-diffusion problems" (Smears and Vohralík 2020). Reasoning: Prager–Synge requires σ symmetric with div σ + f = 0 exactly; with weak symmetry, ‖skw σ_h‖ enters multiplied by a constant from Korn's inequality.
**Fix:** Label elasticity bounds `Estimated` unless the reconstruction is strongly symmetric (Arnold–Winther or Hu–Zhang-type stress elements) and the data are polynomial. Add oscillation and geometry terms explicitly, and add a mixed u–p reconstruction for near-incompressibility with effectivity oracles at ν = 0.49 and 0.4999. Record in each estimator's metadata the theorem behind its `Guaranteed` label.

### R1-08 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, COV
**Section:** Contract §5 `ErrorBudget`; FLX Rust `ErrorBudget`; COV `ErrorBudget<D>`.
**Problem:** There are three incompatible budget definitions: the contract omits temporal, quadrature, floating-point and statistical components, FLX omits input and model form, and COV has four components whose values may be distributions. None defines how interval bounds, confidence-level estimates and distributions combine into the total that checks use.
**Evidence:** Contract §5 lists discretization, solver, geometry conversion, input and tolerance, model form, surrogate and interpolation. FLX's struct has discretization, algebraic, temporal, geometry, quadrature, floating point, statistical and reduction. COV's has discretization, solver, input and model form, with `Estimate::Distribution(Dist<D>)`.
**Fix:** Define one budget in `base` with the union of components (discretization, algebraic, temporal, quadrature, geometry, floating point, input, statistical, model form, reduction or surrogate, interpolation), each an `ErrorBound` or `NotQuantified`. Add a composition rule to the contract: bounds in the same norm add; components with a confidence (statistical, conformal, input distributions) become bounds at a stated per-component level and combine by a union bound, so the total carries confidence 1 − Σα_i; distributions are reported alongside and never summed with intervals. Checks state the confidence level at which their decision holds.

### R1-09 · Major · Targets: FLX, PLT, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-8, determinism mechanism 1, platform notes; PLT "GPU compute" item 5; contract §7.3 ("no implicit FMA contraction anywhere").
**Problem:** WGSL lets implementations reassociate, fuse when "at least as accurate", implement `fma` unfused, flush subnormals and assume that overflow, infinities and NaNs never occur, in which case results become indeterminate values. Emitting no FMA therefore cannot make WGSL kernels share bits with CPU kernels, explicit flushing does not control what the driver flushes or fuses, and in-kernel NaN and infinity checks may be removed, so a diverging `f32` solve can return finite garbage. PLT's per-device double-float self-test cannot show that other pipelines compile without reassociation.
**Evidence:** WGSL specification source, https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb (wgsl/index.bs, cloned 2026-10-11), section "Reassociation and Fusion": "An implementation may reassociate operations." and "An implementation may fuse operations if the transformed expression is at least as accurate as the original formulation." The `fma` accuracy is "Inherited from `x * y + z`", with a note that it "allows an implementation which performs an ordinary multiply ... followed by an ordinary addition". The spec also says "Implementations may assume that overflow, infinities, and NaNs are not present during shader execution" and "Any inputs or outputs of operations listed in [floating-point accuracy] may be flushed to zero"; it defines no `f64` type. FLX D-FLX-8 claims "native, browser and user-form kernels share bits" with "no ... FMA for CPU or WGSL targets".
**Fix:** In contract §7.3, restrict "no implicit FMA contraction" to CPU classes and define GPU classes by the compiled pipeline, keyed by the driver and shader-compiler build plus the pipeline hash. In FLX, drop the shared-bits claim for WGSL; detect non-finite values on the CPU after readback by integer inspection of exponent bits; keep kernels in range by nondimensionalization and argument clamping; use integer arithmetic only for anything that must be exact on GPUs, as D-FLX-18 already does for reductions. In PLT, replace the per-device self-test with a known-answer test for each compiled pipeline, run at creation and after any driver or OS update, with CPU fallback on failure.

### R1-10 · Major · Targets: FLX, PLT, MEC, ELX, CONTRACT
**Section:** Contract §7.1 ("faer for CPU linear algebra"); FLX "Linear algebra layer"; PLT "Third-party numerical libraries"; MEC solver method; ELX D-ELX-13.
**Problem:** faer's dense kernels come from the `gemm` crate, which sizes the reduction block `kc` from cache size and associativity detected at run time and uses FMA microkernels where available. Any product whose inner dimension exceeds that block (at least 512), such as Gram matrices VᵀV in reduced-order modelling and LOBPCG, therefore sums in a machine-dependent order, and FMA and non-FMA microkernels differ across ISA levels. faer as shipped breaks the canonical class, even within one ISA level, for every `Portable` operation that calls it.
**Evidence:** `gemm-common/src/cache.rs`, cloned from https://github.com/sarah-quinones/gemm: `kernel_params` reads `CACHE_INFO`, filled from /sys or CPUID, and computes `auto_kc` from `l1_n_sets` and `l1_assoc`. `gemm-common/src/gemm.rs` defines `v3_fma` with `f64::mul_add`. https://docs.rs/faer/latest/faer/ shows faer 0.24.4 with "cpu feature detection at runtime" and rayon "global parallelism by default", and says nothing on determinism. PLT itself marks faer's behaviour "[unverified]".
**Fix:** Make a canonical linear-algebra crate (or a pinned faer and gemm fork) a phase 0 deliverable owned by PLT and FLX, with fixed machine-independent block sizes, canonical 512-bit lanes, no FMA, sequential or combinator-partitioned updates, and pivot ties broken by index. Unmodified faer runs only in `CpuIsa` mode, and only with `CACHE_INFO` pinned. MEC's constraint solver, ELX's circuit LU and COV's calibration fits use the canonical crate for `Portable` results. Add a determinism-suite case that runs the same factorization under two different `CACHE_INFO` values.

### R1-11 · Major · Targets: FLX, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-17 and "Fidelity modes" (precision row); contract §7.3 (no substitution of GPU results).
**Problem:** Defect correction around `f32` inner solves converges only while each inner solve cuts the true residual by a fixed factor, which fails as κ(A)·u_f32 approaches 1 (high-order elasticity, near-incompressible materials, thin parts, contact), and the draft states no condition or fallback. The outer residual needs an `f64` operator, including `f64` constitutive updates for nonlinear problems. Contract §7.3 forbids GPU results substituting for canonical ones, so on Apple silicon and in browsers every release-grade sign-off is recomputed on the CPU anyway.
**Evidence:** FLX D-FLX-17: "Defect correction attains the outer precision when the inner solver converges". FLX's own Carson–Higham paragraph limits `f32` factorization to κ below 10⁷. Contract §7.3 and PLT D-PLT-20 forbid substitution.
**Fix:** Fall back to canonical CPU whenever the outer residual contracts by less than a factor of 2 per cycle for two cycles, and compute the outer residual on the CPU with canonical `f64` operator and constitutive evaluation. Adopt a "GPU solves, CPU certifies" pattern: pin the GPU solution by `ObjectId`, like an External Op result, and have the canonical CPU recompute its residual and error estimate, so the check outcome is canonical even though the Field bits are not. Amend contract §7.3 to allow a check to rest on a pinned non-canonical Field when its bound is computed canonically.

### R1-12 · Major · Targets: FLX, PLT
**Section:** FLX "Determinism" (cross-class tolerance); PLT "Measuring cross-class agreement".
**Problem:** FLX's agreement tolerance, "the larger of the final residual bound and 100 ulp per value", is wrong three ways: a small residual bounds the forward error only through the condition number (with κ = 10⁶, solutions with relative residual 10⁻¹⁰ can differ by 10⁻⁴), adaptive runs refine different cells in different classes, and ulp tolerances mean nothing near zero. The oracle will fail correct runs and pass wrong ones, and FLX and PLT use different rules.
**Evidence:** FLX: "results agree within a declared tolerance that is the larger of the final residual bound and 100 ulp per value". PLT uses "relative residual of each result in the other class's discretization".
**Fix:** Use one rule in both tabs: classes A and B agree when |q_A − q_B| ≤ E_A + E_B for every checked quantity q, where E is the budget total in q's norm (algebraic, discretization, floating point) plus an absolute floor scaled by the Field norm. Report the ratio |q_A − q_B| / (E_A + E_B); a ratio above 1 also fails the effectivity oracle.

### R1-13 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, PLT
**Section:** Contract principle 4 and the CCR-FLX-7 ruling; FLX "Limits of the machinery".
**Problem:** At bifurcations (buckling, snap-through, symmetric contact, symmetry-breaking flow, electrostatic pull-in) and where several solutions exist (Newton converging to different roots, path-dependent plasticity), rounding differences select different branches. Cross-class agreement then fails by more than any tolerance even without chaos, but the ruling covers only chaotic problems.
**Evidence:** Contract principle 4: "cross-class results agree within declared tolerance (or, for chaotic problems, in declared statistics)". A perfect column has two post-buckling branches of opposite sign, and rounding decides which one Newton follows.
**Fix:** Add to principle 4: "Problems with bifurcations or multiple solutions declare branch selection explicitly (an imperfection or symmetry-breaking load in the input) or declare agreement on branch-invariant quantities (critical loads, mode sets, load–displacement magnitude)." Flux's Newton and arc-length solvers detect bifurcation points (a sign change of the tangent determinant or a zero eigenvalue) and return `Bifurcation { load, modes }` instead of silently picking a branch.

### R1-14 · Major · Targets: FLX, KLN

### R1-15 · Major · Targets: FLX

### R1-17 · Major · Targets: FLX
**Section:** FLX "Krylov methods and preconditioners" (scalar and vector elliptic rows); D-FLX-16.
**Problem:** The default preconditioner on the immersed octree is p-multigrid with Chebyshev–Jacobi smoothing. Jacobi-based smoothers fail on immersed discretizations because small cut cells create problematic eigenvalues, and robust immersed multigrid uses Schwarz-type patch smoothers.
**Evidence:** de Prenter et al., "Multigrid solvers for immersed finite element methods and immersed isogeometric analysis", Comput. Mech. 65 (2020), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00466-019-01796-y: "Elements with small intersections with the physical domain yield problematic eigenvalues", and Sect. 3.4: "Jacobi iterations are not suitable as a smoother for immersed finite elements." Their Schwarz-type smoother gives "mesh-independent and cut-element-independent convergence rates".
**Fix:** Use additive Schwarz smoothers on vertex or cell patches around cut cells, combined with cell aggregation, and keep Chebyshev–Jacobi only on uncut interior cells. Oracle: iteration counts stay within 1.5× across 1,000 random shifts of the geometry against the octree, for p = 1 to 4.

### R1-18 · Major · Targets: FLX, ANV
**Section:** FLX "Immersed methods on octrees" (moment fitting); ANV `GeometryQuery::closest_point`.
**Problem:** Moment fitting at fixed points solves a Vandermonde-type system whose weights can go negative and whose conditioning worsens with degree, undermining mass positivity and cut-cell coercivity at high order; it also needs certified monomial integrals over trimmed NURBS surfaces, itself a hard quadrature problem. The shifted boundary method needs a closest-point vector for every boundary quadrature point, which `Bound(L)` and `Lipschitz(L)` implicits (lattices, smooth blends) do not provide cheaply.
**Evidence:** FLX moment-fitting paragraph. Algoim, https://algoim.github.io/: Saye's methods "construct quadrature schemes with positive weights and can yield high-order accuracy" for implicitly defined domains, and Algoim also provides "High-order accurate algorithms for computing closest points on implicitly-defined surfaces" (Saye, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 37(2), 2015; J. Comput. Phys. 448, 2022).
**Fix:** For cut cells of implicit and polynomial geometry, use Saye-type dimension-reduction quadrature (a height-function reformulation with interval-certified monotonicity, which Anvil's implicit evaluator already supplies). Keep moment fitting only with non-negative least squares and a conditioning check. Specify certified closest-point projection for implicits (Newton on f inside an interval-certified basin) and include its cost in the 1 s budget. Algoim's page states no licence, so reimplement from the papers unless the repository licence is permissive.

### R3-09 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, FLX, ING, AGT, UIX

### R1-55 · Minor · Targets: FLX, PLT
