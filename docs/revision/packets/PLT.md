## Second-review findings

### R7-12 · Major · Targets: PLT, FLX
**Section:** PLT D-PLT-35; FLX "Verification, validity and agreement" (FLX-O-ERR-10)
**Problem:** The R1-12 fix does not hold: requiring |q_A − q_B| ≤ E_A + E_B lets a class drift by the whole discretization error before anyone notices. Plans are hardware-independent, so when both classes solve the same discretization the discretization error is common to both and cancels in q_A − q_B. Only the algebraic, floating-point and step-control parts differ by class, and they are orders of magnitude smaller. A GPU pipeline that is wrong by 3% still "agrees" when the discretization part is 4%. The same rule fires false severity-1 incidents: with `Estimated` parts at 99%, about 2% of correct comparisons exceed the ratio 1, and a nightly corpus of thousands of quantities produces dozens of false incidents.
**Evidence:** D-PLT-35: "E is q's `ErrorBudget` total in q's norm plus an absolute floor"; "a ratio above 1 also fails the owning tab's effectivity oracle". FLX: "Plans depend only on their inputs, never on hardware". Contract 7.3: "A nightly divergence … opens a severity-1 determinism incident".
**Fix:** Rewrite D-PLT-35: when both classes ran the same resolved `SolvePlan` and the same adaptive marks, the tolerance is the sum of each class's algebraic, floating-point and temporal-control parts plus the absolute floor. Full budgets apply only when marks differ, and diverging marks are reported as their own finding. An incident opens when a `Guaranteed` or algebraic comparison fails once, or an `Estimated` comparison fails on two consecutive nightly runs.

### R7-32 · Minor · Targets: COV, PLT
**Section:** COV Open questions 9
**Problem:** COV leaves open whether emulated `f64` (double-float arithmetic) on WebGPU can serve `SignOff` for `F64Required` models. The open question invites a path that the contract already rules out, and it keeps alive a capability that cannot be made reliable.
**Evidence:** Double-float arithmetic rests on error-free transformations (TwoSum, TwoProd), which need correctly rounded operations evaluated in program order. The WebGPU conformance primer lets devices flush subnormals to zero (https://kdab.searchfox.org/firefox-main/source/dom/webgpu/tests/cts/checkout/docs/fp_primer.md), and contract 7.5, after R1-09, records that WGSL may reassociate and fuse. D-FLX-17 already requires canonical CPU outcomes at `SignOff`.
**Fix (answers COV open question 9):** No. `F64Required` models never run in emulated `f64` on WebGPU. `SignOff` runs on CPU classes, and browser `Interactive` runs on the Wasm CPU path or remotely. Delete the question and record the decision under D-COV-23.

### R7-38 · Minor · Targets: FLX, PLT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-9, FLX-O-KER-6
**Problem:** The user-form target "at most 2× the ahead-of-time time on AVX2" cannot hold for compute-bound kernels. Wasm SIMD128 has half AVX2's width, so 2× is the floor before bounds checks, fuel metering (up to 10% by contract 7.4) and NaN canonicalization. Only memory-bound kernels can meet it.
**Evidence:** D-FLX-9: "user forms target at most 2× the ahead-of-time time on AVX2 and 4× on AVX-512". Contract 7.4: "fuel and stack metering ≤ 10%".

### R8-48 · Minor · Targets: ANV, PLT, KLN
**Section:** ANV Open questions 1 (D-ANV-18)
**Problem:** The CI cost of keeping every version executable is open.
**Evidence:** ANV open question 1: "PLT and Kiln must confirm the CI cost."

### R9-06 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, FLX, ANV, PLT
**Section:** CONTRACT 4 ("frozen by content hash"); KLN "Scope and the operation contract" (D-KLN-2); ANV "Behavior versions" (D-ANV-18); FLX "Kernels, back ends and determinism"
**Problem:** Three mechanisms define what keeps an operation version's bits fixed, and the one Kiln enforces is the weakest. ANV freezes each version's source modules by hash; KLN accepts any code change whose outputs on a conformance corpus are unchanged; FLX only states the policy. A change in a shared result-affecting crate (`linalg`, the `kernels` back ends that compile std forms ahead of time, `simd`) that alters outputs outside the corpus keeps the key and silently poisons every cache that holds the old bits, across tenants.
**Evidence:** Contract 4: "Each operation version is frozen by content hash once released." ANV l.52 (D-ANV-18): "Each operation version, and every internal module it uses (for example `ssi@4`), is frozen by source hash". KLN l.37 (D-KLN-2): "Each operation version registers a digest of its outputs over a conformance corpus, and CI fails when code changes the digest without a version bump." FLX l.344: "Any output-bit change … is a new behavior version", with no freeze mechanism.
**Fix:** Contract 4 adopts ANV's rule for every operation: a released version freezes its source closure, meaning its own modules plus versioned modules of every result-affecting first-party crate it calls (`linalg@n`, `kernels` back end `@n`, `simd` horizontal ops `@n`), recorded as a closure hash in the operation registry. A change to a frozen module creates a new module version used only by new operation versions. KLN's registration refuses a released version whose closure hash changed; the corpus fingerprint remains a second check. Toolchain and `detmath` upgrades keep ANV's golden-hash rule, which correct rounding makes sound for `detmath` only. KLN-O-FPRINT-1 adds: a seeded change in `linalg` outside the corpus is refused by the closure hash.
