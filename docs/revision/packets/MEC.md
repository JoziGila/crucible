### R1-02 · Critical · Targets: FLX, COV, ANV, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** FLX D-FLX-12, D-FLX-13, "Immersed methods on octrees", plan table; contract §7.1 (Flux default discretization).
**Problem:** The default immersed volumetric octree does not fit the thin walls, lattices and frames of golden products 2, 4, 5 and 8: at two cells across, an inclined wall's shifted-boundary surrogate domain breaks into disconnected cubes, most cells in a thin wall are cut, and DOF counts exceed the 200,000-DOF interactive budget by one to three orders of magnitude. The escape hatch, IGA shells for walls thinner than 5% of the bounding size, uses a global criterion and leaves ribs, bosses and junctions unhandled. Flux has no beam or frame discretization, so welded frames, steel platforms and strut lattices have no efficient model.
**Evidence:** FLX: "The octree refines until every thin section has at least two cells across its thickness"; "Cut cells are a lower-dimensional fraction of all cells, so they do not dominate cost"; plan row "thin walls with thickness below 5% of the bounding size". For a wall with normal (1,1,1)/√3, a cube of side h spans √3·h through the wall, so a 2h-thick wall contains it only if its centre lies within about 0.13h of the mid-surface. A 200 × 150 × 50 mm enclosure with 2 mm walls holds 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ m³ of material: 1.5 × 10⁶ cells and about 3.6 × 10⁷ DOFs for p = 2 elasticity at 4 cells across, and 4.6 × 10⁶ DOFs at 2 cells across. A 100 × 60 × 40 mm bracket of 5 mm BCC cells with 0.8 mm struts needs about 8 × 10⁶ cells (2 × 10⁸ DOFs) at 4 cells per strut diameter. MEC "Weldments and structural frames" builds swept HEA members; no Flux table lists beam elements, and COV mentions "shell and beam FEM" only for phase 7 civil structures.
**Fix:** (1) Add dimensionally reduced discretizations in Flux phases 1–2: Timoshenko and Vlasov (warping) beams for members and struts, Reissner–Mindlin shells on Anvil mid-surfaces for walls, and rigid and Nitsche-based solid–shell–beam couplings as mixed-dimensional `dI` interfaces. (2) Replace the 5% rule with a local criterion: t/L_local ≤ 0.05 for Kirchhoff–Love and ≤ 0.2 for Reissner–Mindlin, where L_local is the smallest of the span between supports, the radius of curvature and the distance to the nearest load or feature; regions that fail stay solid. (3) Require at least 4 cells across any section for the shifted boundary method and check surrogate-domain connectivity against the Body's topology, falling back to aggregated cut cells with a typed note on mismatch. (4) Use beam-lattice models with node corrections for full-resolution lattice analysis and keep homogenization for design (R1-26). (5) Amend contract §7.1 to "immersed octree by default for compact solids; dimensionally reduced models for thin walls, members and struts, chosen by a stated local criterion."

### R2-02 · Critical · Targets: ANV, ING, KLN, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** ANV Symbolic perturbation; Memory layout; Determinism rules; ING Entities and call sites; MEC Solver method, Patterns and mirror (`by_density`), D-MEC-22; KLN CRDT over the syntax tree; Storage format
**Problem:** EntityIds hash OpSites whose statement identity is a Loro node id, which is not a function of the source text and is dropped by `crucible export git`. Anvil and MEC nevertheless use EntityIds to order, tie-break and seed geometric computation, so meshes, `by_density` placements, reduction orders, solver decompositions and variation statistics depend on invisible metadata. Identical text can give different bits, a reviewer cannot predict geometry from a diff, and a design exported to Git and re-imported may not rebuild identically, which breaks D-MEC-5 ("the result must not depend on edit history, only on source") and the exit guarantee.
**Evidence:** ANV: SoS "perturbation order is the canonical vertex order of the encoded Body (face-group EntityId, then local index)"; row order "Morton code ... then EntityId"; BVH "EntityId tie-breaks"; independent sets "priority is a hash of the element's deterministic id". MEC: `by_density` "seeded from EntityIds"; decomposition "Ties break by EntityId"; Sobol "scramble seed derived from the design's EntityIds"; witness values "pseudo-random rationals derived from EntityIds". ING: statement identity is "The BlockId of the enclosing statement block"; external edits "match blocks by GumTree, so most BlockIds survive". KLN: "Block identities are Loro tree node ids"; "crucible export git writes Ingot text and lockfiles".
**Fix:** Add a label-invariance rule to contract 7.3: an operation's geometry, connectivity, Field values and statistics are invariant under any injective renaming of input EntityIds and OpSites; only label sections may change. Implement it with canonical orders from geometry and topology (box minimum, box maximum, entity type, defining parameters; distinct entities of a valid Body never tie on all of them), SoS indices and independent-set priorities from that order or from geometric content hashes, and seeds for `by_density`, Sobol scrambles and witness values from hashes of argument values and input geometry. Add oracle ANV-O-LABEL-1: rebuild every golden product with all BlockIds re-randomized and require identical geometry hashes (label sections excluded) and identical check values.

### R1-07 · Major · Targets: FLX, COV, MEC
**Section:** FLX open questions 3 and 11; FLX phasing (phase 1 sign-off on immersed octrees).
**Problem:** Phase 1 sign-off of golden products 1 and 2 runs on immersed octrees, where every bound is `Estimated` (OQ11). The policy deciding when `Estimated` bounds may support a `Pass` is still open (OQ3), so the phase 1 gate depends on an undecided rule.
**Evidence:** FLX OQ3: "A human policy must say which checks may pass on `Estimated` bounds"; OQ11: "immersed results carry `Estimated` bounds"; phase 1 row: "Golden product 1 checks decided at sign-off".
**Fix:** Decide the rule in phase 0 and put it in the contract: an `Estimated` bound may support `Pass` at sign-off only when (1) the estimator's effectivity on the problem class's oracle corpus has a stated 99th-percentile underestimate factor η99, (2) the check multiplies the bound by η99, and (3) the margin exceeds the inflated bound; otherwise `Undecided`. η99 tables are versioned data pinned in `ingot.lock` (R1-14). Owners: FLX keeps the corpus, COV maps model classes, MEC decides which mechanical checks need it.

### R1-10 · Major · Targets: FLX, PLT, MEC, ELX, CONTRACT
**Section:** Contract §7.1 ("faer for CPU linear algebra"); FLX "Linear algebra layer"; PLT "Third-party numerical libraries"; MEC solver method; ELX D-ELX-13.
**Problem:** faer's dense kernels come from the `gemm` crate, which sizes the reduction block `kc` from cache size and associativity detected at run time and uses FMA microkernels where available. Any product whose inner dimension exceeds that block (at least 512), such as Gram matrices VᵀV in reduced-order modelling and LOBPCG, therefore sums in a machine-dependent order, and FMA and non-FMA microkernels differ across ISA levels. faer as shipped breaks the canonical class, even within one ISA level, for every `Portable` operation that calls it.
**Evidence:** `gemm-common/src/cache.rs`, cloned from https://github.com/sarah-quinones/gemm: `kernel_params` reads `CACHE_INFO`, filled from /sys or CPUID, and computes `auto_kc` from `l1_n_sets` and `l1_assoc`. `gemm-common/src/gemm.rs` defines `v3_fma` with `f64::mul_add`. https://docs.rs/faer/latest/faer/ shows faer 0.24.4 with "cpu feature detection at runtime" and rayon "global parallelism by default", and says nothing on determinism. PLT itself marks faer's behaviour "[unverified]".
**Fix:** Make a canonical linear-algebra crate (or a pinned faer and gemm fork) a phase 0 deliverable owned by PLT and FLX, with fixed machine-independent block sizes, canonical 512-bit lanes, no FMA, sequential or combinator-partitioned updates, and pivot ties broken by index. Unmodified faer runs only in `CpuIsa` mode, and only with `CACHE_INFO` pinned. MEC's constraint solver, ELX's circuit LU and COV's calibration fits use the canonical crate for `Portable` results. Add a determinism-suite case that runs the same factorization under two different `CACHE_INFO` values.

### R1-36 · Major · Targets: FLX, COV, MEC
**Section:** FLX "Uncertainty quantification" methods table and "Propagating `Tol<T>`"; COV Material uncertainty.
**Problem:** Sobol indices assume independent inputs, but `Tol<T>` has correlation groups and Material uncertainty has correlation matrices, so contributions are ill-defined exactly where correlation exists. Rare-event estimation for high yields has only FORM and importance sampling, in phase 4; epistemic quantities (model-form intervals, unknown distributions) have no representation separate from aleatory ones; and multilevel Monte Carlo with per-sample adaptive meshes breaks the fixed level hierarchy.
**Evidence:** FLX Sobol row. Contract §5: `Tol<T>` statistics include "correlation groups". COV `Uncertainty`: "with a correlation matrix across properties".
**Fix:** Add Shapley effects for dependent inputs and grouped Sobol indices per correlation group; subset simulation and cross-entropy importance sampling for failure probabilities below 10⁻³, in phase 3; interval representation of epistemic inputs, propagated by nested sampling and reported as probability boxes; and tolerance-based multilevel Monte Carlo levels when the plan adapts per sample.

### R1-37 · Major · Targets: MEC, FLX, MFG
**Section:** MEC "Stack-up and variation analysis", D-MEC-22; FLX "Propagating `Tol<T>`".
**Problem:** Default distributions conflict (MEC uses a normal with limits at ±3σ, FLX a uniform within limits), so for half-width T the RSS standard deviations are T/3 against T/√3, 73% apart, and the two tabs report different yields for one design. Neither models mean shift or process drift, which makes RSS unconservative. D-MEC-22 justifies quasi-Monte Carlo by smoothness, but yields are expectations of indicator functions, where quasi-Monte Carlo loses most of its advantage; and FLX's Karhunen–Loève boundary perturbations duplicate MEC's skin-model shapes.
**Evidence:** MEC: "Contributor distributions default to normal with the zone limits at ±3σ". FLX: "uniform within limits by default, normal with ±3σ at the limits when the process capability is declared". MEC: "quasi-Monte Carlo converges faster than pseudo-random sampling for smooth measurements [unverified]".
**Fix:** One default owned by MEC and used by FLX: uniform within limits when no process data exist, and normal with mean shift and spread from Cp and Cpk in MFG process profiles when they do. Report an RSS variant with a Bender-type inflation factor. Estimate yields with smoothed indicators or conditional sampling and put the repeated-scramble interval in the statistical component of the budget. FLX consumes MEC's skin-model samples as `Field<Faces, Displacement>` instead of its own expansion.

### R1-38 · Major · Targets: MEC, ANV
**Section:** MEC "Solver method" step 4 and "Diagnosis" (rank-revealing QR).
**Problem:** MEC uses "sparse column-pivoted QR from faer" above 64 unknowns and rank-revealing QR for degree-of-freedom diagnosis. faer documents its sparse QR only as simplicial and supernodal, with a column elimination tree fixed before numerics, and no rank-revealing or numerically pivoted mode.
**Evidence:** https://docs.rs/faer/latest/faer/sparse/linalg/qr/index.html lists `col_etree` ("column elimination tree"), `postorder`, a "simplicial factorization module" and a "supernodal factorization module", and says nothing about rank.
**Fix:** Use dense rank-revealing QR or SVD per decomposed block, which the Dulmage–Mendelsohn split keeps small. Above a size cap, run sparse QR followed by a rank-revealing pass on R (a condition estimator plus deflation), implemented in the canonical crate of R1-10. Add a diagnosis oracle with known rank deficiencies for blocks of 100 to 2,000 unknowns.

### R2-07 · Major · Targets: ANV, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** ANV Blends (parameter and stage tables), State of the art (Blends); MEC Dress-up features; contract 7.1 (blends as a research program)
**Problem:** Phase 1 blending is constant radius with `KeepEdge` overflow and equal-radius three-edge corners on "analytic and NURBS supports"; blends whose supports are earlier blends wait for phase 4, yet the default overflow is `RollOver` (stage 2). Phase 1 users will fail on unequal radii at a corner, mixed-convexity corners (a boss-base fillet meeting a vertical-edge fillet), vertices of valence four or more, fillets that must roll onto a neighbor, filleting the top loop after the vertical edges, chamfer-fillet corners, chains across smooth edges and variable radius. OCCT, the open reference the draft benchmarks against, already offers linear and law-driven radii on tangent chains with corner fills.
**Evidence:** ANV stage 1 row; `overflow` default `RollOver`; stage 4 "Blends on blends (rolling over earlier blends)"; MEC `overflow: Overflow = Auto`; ANV target "above OCCT 8.0's at stage 1" versus MEC "target 97% success at phase 2"; OCCT `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet` reference (https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_fillet_a_p_i___make_fillet.html, opened): `Add(R1, R2, E)` sets "a linear radius evolution law", `Add(L, E)` takes a law, `Add(E)` "builds a contour of tangent edges", failed corners are reported per vertex.
**Fix:** Re-scope stage 1: supports include Anvil's procedural blend, offset and swept surfaces, so sequential fillets work; `RollOver` overflow; valence-3 corners with unequal radii and mixed convexity by setback patches; chamfer-fillet corners; termination at smooth edges; linearly varying radius. Leave valence ≥ 4, Field-driven radii and G2/G3 sections to later stages and amend the contract's ordering to match. Until `RollOver` ships, both tabs default to `KeepEdge`. Build the blend benchmark in phase 0 (recognize and remove fillets on ABC parts, re-fillet with the recorded radii) and state one target per phase in both tabs.

### R2-09 · Major · Targets: ANV, KLN, PLT, UIX, MEC
**Section:** ANV Performance targets, Fidelity levels; MEC Performance budget; UIX Coarse-first preview pipeline; ING Previews, drags and incomplete code; PLT Budgets
**Problem:** The 2 s exact rebuild of a 200-feature part after an early edit and the 100 ms coarse preview "with dependent features updated" are not supported by any mechanism in the drafts. (2) State the 2 s target for `Interactive` and add a SignOff target (for example 10 s). ### R2-11 · Major · Targets: ANV, MEC, ING
**Section:** ANV Transform, pattern, mirror; MEC Patterns and mirror; ING Kiln-held values (`.moved(by:)`)
**Problem:** `anvil::transform` keeps EntityIds for rigid motions and reflections, and MEC's `mirror` and Ingot's `move` lower to it. Combining a Body with a moved or mirrored copy of itself, the commonest symmetry idiom (`half + mirror(half, plane.yz)`), or two Instances of one Part in an assembly-level feature, puts identical EntityIds into one Boolean, which fails with `EntityIdCollision` or makes every query on the seed resolve to both copies. Roles also flip meaning under reflection (`side(left)` lands on the right).
**Evidence:** ANV transform row "EntityIds kept: identity for rigid motions, `Modified { aspects: Geometry }` for scaling and reflection"; the separate-id guarantee exists only for `anvil::pattern` ("an original and its mirror copy never share EntityIds"); "Within one Body, a duplicate EntityId fails the operation with `Internal::EntityIdCollision`"; MEC patterns and mirror use "`anvil::transform`, `anvil::boolean` (n-ary)".
**Fix:** Separate placement from copying. A transform whose source value is used again in the program (Ingot knows this from data flow) or that is written as `copy` creates ids `EID(kind, site, role: copy, [source id])` with a new `Copied { from, into }` record that lineage follows only on request (`seed.face(top).copies()`); a motion that replaces the Body in the data flow keeps ids. Reflection records `Mirrored`, so role queries can say `side(left, mirrored: true)`. Assembly-level features prefix ids with the Instance path.

### R2-12 · Major · Targets: ING, MEC, ANV, CONTRACT
**Section:** ING Entities and call sites (Loop indices); Patterns, loops and identity; MEC Patterns and mirror (`skip: [Int]`, `at_points`, `fill`, `by_density`); ANV Rust API (`OpSite`)
**Problem:** `OpSite` uses the positional iteration index by default and keyed iteration is opt-in; patterns name copies and skip instances by index. Patterns driven by geometry (`at_points`, `fill`, `by_density`, `along`) and loops over query results (`for e in body.edges(convex)`) therefore rename every later copy, retarget skip lists and break anchors when one element is inserted or removed. That is positional indexing under another name, contrary to the contract's "No positional indexing".
**Evidence:** ING "Loop indices | The iteration index, or the key of a `keyed` collection"; "`for x in keyed(holes, by: |h| h.label)` uses the key in place of the index" (opt-in); ANV `OpSite { path, indices: Box<[u32]> }`; MEC `skip: [Int] = []`; `by_density` runs Lloyd iterations that move every seed.
**Fix:** Make keys the default. Iteration over `Entity<K>` lists or resolved queries keys by EntityId; transform generators return keyed lists (`linear` keys `(i, j)` from the seed, `circular` the angular index, `at_points` the point's anchor EntityId, `fill` lattice coordinates from a declared origin, `by_density` each seed's initial key before Lloyd steps); plain integer ranges keep integer keys. Replace `skip: [Int]` with `skip: [Key]` and `OpSite.indices: [u32]` with `[SiteKey]`. The language service warns on positional keys over lists whose length depends on geometry.

### R2-14 · Major · Targets: ANV, MEC, CONTRACT
**Section:** ANV Representation lattice (D-ANV-2), Conversions (Implicit → BRep); MEC Lattices and Field-driven geometry; contract section 5 `Body`
**Problem:** By the lattice, a B-rep part joined with an implicit lattice becomes an `Implicit` Body, which has no faces, edges, attributes or B-rep EntityIds, and in phase 1 converts back only to facet faces. Golden product 2 therefore loses its exact mounting holes, `HoleSpec` attributes, GD&T targets and drawings, although semantic PMI requires exact geometry and post-print machining of interfaces needs exact faces. ### R2-15 · Major · Targets: MEC, ANV, PLT
**Section:** MEC Solver method (D-MEC-4), Platform notes, Oracles (MEC-O-SK-4), Molded and cast part design (D-MEC-18)
**Problem:** `anvil::solve_constraints` and `anvil::thickness` produce geometry and geometric Fields, which the contract restricts to the `Portable` grade, but MEC specifies faer sparse QR (runtime CPU-feature dispatch and rayon parallelism on by default, no reproducibility statement), says "Fused multiply-add use is fixed per platform class", accepts 1e-12 relative agreement across classes, and computes thickness Fields on the GPU with CPU refinement only near thresholds. ### R2-16 · Major · Targets: ING, MEC, ANV
**Section:** ING Sketches and constraints; MEC Solver method (step 4) and Diagnosis
**Problem:** A solved sketch point is returned as `Approx<Point3>` "whose bound comes from the solver's converged residual", but a residual is not a forward error bound: the position error is about ‖J⁻¹‖·‖r‖, large for nearly tangent arcs, nearly parallel lines and near-singular mates. A `Guaranteed` bound derived this way lets `Truth` decide checks wrongly. The convergence rule "below one hundredth of the smallest Anvil entity tolerance" (about 10⁻¹² m for a 0.1 m sketch) also fails on ill-conditioned but valid sketches.
**Evidence:** ING "Reading `s.c.center` after the solve returns `Approx<Point3>`, whose bound comes from the solver's converged residual"; MEC step 4 "Convergence requires the scaled residual below one hundredth of the smallest Anvil entity tolerance involved"; Anvil already certifies roots with Krawczyk elsewhere.
**Fix:** After each well-determined block converges, run a Krawczyk (or Kantorovich) test on the scaled system; the radius of the proven box is the `Guaranteed` bound and certifies a unique solution on the chosen branch. If the test fails, the bound is `Estimated` (an estimate of ‖J⁻¹‖ times the residual) and the report says why. Replace the absolute convergence rule with "residual small enough that the certified box radius is below the smallest entity tolerance", else `IllConditioned { block, condition }` with fixes.

### R2-20 · Major · Targets: ANV, MEC, UIX
**Section:** ANV Direct edits and defeaturing (`move_faces`); MEC Direct edits (D-MEC-9); UIX Push-pull
**Problem:** MEC promises that `anvil::move_faces` "regrows or removes blends", but Anvil's contract only re-intersects neighbors. Moving a face next to fillets, which is nearly every face of a real part, then fails or leaves blends with the wrong radius and broken tangency. ### R3-29 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ING, MEC, ELX, FLX
**Section:** CONTRACT 5 (`Quantity<D>`, ruling ING-7); ING "Dimensions" (D-ING-3)
**Problem:** Making angle a base dimension while keeping torque in N·m leaves the basic laws of rotation ill-typed: power τω, work τθ, τ = Iα and kinetic energy ½Iω² each carry stray radians. ω = √(k/I) yields A^(−1/2)·T⁻¹, which rational exponents accept without complaint. **Fix:** Choose one consistent system in phase 0 and publish it as a std table. ### R1-47 · Minor · Targets: MEC, ANV

**Problem:** Convergence is tested on the scaled residual, but near-singular configurations (near-tangency) have a position error of roughly residual / σ_min, so a small residual can hide errors far above entity tolerance.
