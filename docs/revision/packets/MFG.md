### R4-02 · Critical · Targets: MFG
**Section:** Machine models; Controllers and post-processors; Material removal and machine collision simulation; Oracles.
**Problem:** Collision verification checks the toolpath, not how the control moves between programmed points. The `Controller` record has no fields for rapid traverse interpolation (straight line or each axis at its own rate), rotary rapid direction and unwinding, TCP state during rapids and tool changes, reference-return intermediate points, canned-cycle retract planes, or cutter-compensation entry and look-ahead. Those are where verified programs crash in practice, so the claim that rapids are checked "with the same rigor as cuts" is unsafe as specified.
**Evidence:** MFG: `controller: Controller, // dialect, options, capability flags`; "The model describes nominal kinematics only."; "Rapid-move safety | Rapids checked against stock and fixture with the same rigor as cuts | `Collision` | As above | 1". ISO 6983-1 defines program format and address words (https://www.iso.org/standard/34608.html); positioning (G00) fixes the end point, and the path between points is control-specific [unverified per control; a Fanuc parameter, for example, selects linear or non-linear positioning]. Nothing in the seeded crash corpus specification plants controller-semantics crashes.
**Fix:** Add `MotionSemantics` to `Controller`: `rapid: Linear | PerAxis { rates }`, `rotary_rapid: Shortest | Directional | Unwind`, `tcp_in_rapid: Bool`, `reference_return: ViaIntermediate`, `cycle_retract: { initial_plane, r_plane }`, `cutter_comp: { lookahead_blocks, entry_rule }`, `tool_change_envelope: Body`. Collision simulation and the independent re-interpreter both execute the posted NC text under these semantics, never the pre-post toolpath. Where a semantic is undeclared, the simulator sweeps a conservative envelope (for a rapid, the joint-space box between the endpoints) and reports `Unsupported` instead of passing. Posts get a rule: convert G0 to G1 at a declared rapid-equivalent feed wherever the conservative envelope comes closer to stock or fixture than the declared clearance. Add to the crash corpus: dog-leg rapids through clamps, rotary unwinding through the fixture, reference return through the part, wrong retract plane over a clamp, cutter-compensation entry gouge, tool change inside the work envelope. Gate: zero false negatives on that corpus for each controller family before its post ships.

### R1-37 · Major · Targets: MEC, FLX, MFG
**Section:** MEC "Stack-up and variation analysis", D-MEC-22; FLX "Propagating `Tol<T>`".
**Problem:** Default distributions conflict (MEC uses a normal with limits at ±3σ, FLX a uniform within limits), so for half-width T the RSS standard deviations are T/3 against T/√3, 73% apart, and the two tabs report different yields for one design. Neither models mean shift or process drift, which makes RSS unconservative. D-MEC-22 justifies quasi-Monte Carlo by smoothness, but yields are expectations of indicator functions, where quasi-Monte Carlo loses most of its advantage; and FLX's Karhunen–Loève boundary perturbations duplicate MEC's skin-model shapes.
**Evidence:** MEC: "Contributor distributions default to normal with the zone limits at ±3σ". FLX: "uniform within limits by default, normal with ±3σ at the limits when the process capability is declared". MEC: "quasi-Monte Carlo converges faster than pseudo-random sampling for smooth measurements [unverified]".
**Fix:** One default owned by MEC and used by FLX: uniform within limits when no process data exist, and normal with mean shift and spread from Cp and Cpk in MFG process profiles when they do. Report an RSS variant with a Bender-type inflation factor. Estimate yields with smoothed indicators or conditional sampling and put the repeated-scramble interval in the statistical component of the budget. FLX consumes MEC's skin-model samples as `Field<Faces, Displacement>` instead of its own expansion.

### R1-41 · Major · Targets: MFG, COV
**Section:** MFG "Injection molding: process simulation", D-MFG-15; COV "Injection moulding".
**Problem:** Fibre orientation uses Folgar–Tucker, which overpredicts orientation kinetics in concentrated short-fibre melts. Warpage from "residual stress from in-cavity cooling" omits the frozen-layer pressure history, the mould constraint and crystallization kinetics, which dominate warpage accuracy. The sign-off free-surface method is unnamed.
**Evidence:** COV: "Folgar–Tucker fibre orientation with IBOF closure". MFG warp row: "Residual stress from in-cavity cooling and orientation-dependent shrinkage". Reasoning: reduced-strain-closure and anisotropic-rotary-diffusion variants exist because Folgar–Tucker changes orientation too fast in concentrated suspensions.
**Fix:** Use ARD-RSC orientation with a fitted closure, keeping Folgar–Tucker as the L0 rung. Model warpage from residual stress along the pvT path with frozen-layer pressure and mould constraint, add crystallization kinetics for semicrystalline grades, and offer viscoelastic relaxation after ejection. Name the fill method (a level-set front on the octree for the Stokes-regime melt, with mass checks) and validate against GP5 short shots and warpage plus a published plaque benchmark.

### R2-24 · Major · Targets: MFG, ANV, PLT
**Section:** MFG Stock models (D-MFG-6); Material removal and machine collision simulation
**Problem:** Gouge and leftover checks read the narrow-band grid stock, so their accuracy is the grid spacing, yet the gouge oracle requires every planted gouge to be found. For golden product 1 at a 5 µm spacing, a 100 × 100 × 30 mm part has about 1.3 × 10⁹ surface cells and about 8 × 10⁹ band cells (roughly 60 GB of f64), against a 1 GiB memory target; at a spacing the laptop can hold (20 µm or more), shallower gouges go undetected.
**Evidence:** D-MFG-6 "In-process stock is an `Implicit` Body backed by a sparse narrow-band distance grid at a declared resolution"; "Gouge and leftover | Signed distance from simulated stock to the exact design B-rep ... Seeded gouge corpus: every planted gouge detected"; PLT "Memory with golden product 1 open | Under 1 GiB in total". Arithmetic: surface area about 0.032 m² divided by (5 µm)², times a band of ±3 cells.
**Fix:** Detect gouges independently of the stock: for every cutting move, compute the certified minimum distance between the tool's exact swept envelope and the design B-rep (`anvil::distance` against `anvil::swept_volume` per move, or `tool_contact` along it), which is exact and needs no stock. Use the stock only for leftover, rest machining and display, with adaptive refinement near the design surface and a stated `ErrorBound` per setup. Add tri-dexel stock with exact dexel endpoints as a candidate in the phase 1 benchmark.

### R4-05 · Major · Targets: MFG, AGT
**Section:** MFG Approval and machine safety; Machine and MES connectivity (program transfer); Platform notes.
**Problem:** Approval binding ends where Crucible hands the file over. A control cannot verify a signature, operators routinely edit programs, offsets and feeds at the control, and the controller's actual tool lengths and work offsets decide whether the simulated program is the one that runs; none of this is captured. (5) Oracles: recorded edit sessions yield Proposals with exact diffs; a seeded offset-mismatch corpus is blocked 100%.

ESPR recital 38 requires a back-up copy "through a digital product passport service provider that is an independent third party" (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401781).
**Fix:** Cite EUR-Lex for the act numbers or mark them [unverified]. Add a capability, "Back-up copy", that mirrors each published passport to a second, independent DPP service provider named in the delegated-act package, with its failure behavior and oracle.
