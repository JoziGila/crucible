### R13-04 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, COV, FLX, MFG, ELX
**Section:** Contract 13.4 (prerequisite rule, "the spec check enforces this across tabs"); COV coverage matrix rows; FLX "Eigenvalue problems", "Krylov methods and preconditioners"
**Problem:** Contract 13.4 says nothing is phased before its prerequisites and that the spec check enforces this. The checker has no such rule, and rows outside 13.4 break it: (a) COV rotordynamics is phase 2 (Campbell diagrams, rotating-frame FEM), but the gyroscopic and damped eigenproblems arrive in FLX phase 3 ("Damping and flutter linearizations follow in phase 3"). (b) COV linear acoustics FEM with PML is phase 3, but FLX's indefinite Helmholtz solvers (complex LDLᵀ, shifted-Laplacian multigrid) are phase 4. (c) COV's signal and power integrity row is phase 3 with full-wave extraction, but FLX full-wave FEM is phase 4 and ELX's own post-layout extraction is phase 4. (d) COV's welding row puts the phase 4 transient model "with phase transformation", while JMAK and Koistinen–Marburger arrive in phase 6 (heat treatment, ICME). (e) R13-07: allowables in phase 3 against phase 1 strut basis values.
**Evidence:** `docs/tools/check.py` only checks that each capability row has a phase (line 298, "has no phase"); it never compares phases across tabs or against 13.4. FLX's linear-algebra table lists "Indefinite Maxwell and Helmholtz | Complex LDLᵀ; optimized Schwarz; shifted-Laplacian multigrid | 4".
**Fix:** Add a "Requires" field to every capability row that names another tab's capability, and have `check.py` compare phases. Contract 13.4 can stay a table without a Needs column, because the field lives in the tabs. Add 13.4 rows: structural frequency response and damped or gyroscopic eigenproblems at phase 2 (owner FLX; also COV and MEC), and Helmholtz FEM solvers at phase 3 (owner FLX; also COV and ELX). Drop "with phase transformation" from COV's phase 4 weld row, or move JMAK and Koistinen–Marburger to phase 4. Make COV's signal and power integrity row "3 quasi-static, 4 full-wave".

### R13-05 · Major · Targets: COV, FLX, MEC, MFG
**Section:** COV "Dynamics and stability" (D-COV-30); FLX "Calibrated estimates" (`lattice.buckling`); MEC "Design checks" table
**Problem:** Almost every GP2 strut lands in D-COV-30's `Refuse` zone, so the phase 1 `lattice.buckling` class decides almost nothing. The escalation rungs (Johnson, tangent modulus, Perry–Robertson) have no FLX capability, calibration class or MEC check family in phase 1. An LPBF strut of about 1 mm with L/d ≈ 4 has slenderness 4L/d ≈ 16, so σ_cr,Euler = π²E/λ² ≈ 2,700 MPa for AlSi10Mg (E ≈ 70 GPa). That is far above any proportional limit, so the ratio is about 10 or more against D-COV-30's `Refuse` threshold of 1. For such stocky struts, compressive yield and plastic collapse govern, not buckling.
**Evidence:** D-COV-30: "Calibrated while the inelasticity ratio … stays below 0.5, Extrapolated to 1 and `Refuse` beyond." MEC's table names only `lattice.buckling` and `elasticity.eigen` for buckling.
**Fix:** In phase 1, decide strut compression with a strut-strength check: the localized axial stress against the as-built compressive strength, reduced by a Johnson–Perry–Robertson factor computed from the measured waviness. It reads `lattice.strut_stress`, so it needs no new class. Run eigen-buckling (`lattice.buckling`) only on members with ratio below 1, where it governs global lattice instability. Add the strength check to MEC's table and FLX's beam-lattice row. Have MFG's coupons include compression tests of single struts at the GP2 diameters.

### R13-07 · Major · Targets: COV, MFG, CONTRACT
**Section:** COV "Material databases and licensing" capability table (the Allowables row, phase 3); "Statistical basis" (phase 1); 13.4 as-built strut calibration (phase 1)
**Problem:** GP2's strut-scale Materials have no specification limit, so D-COV-13 falls back to B-basis, and computing B-basis values is a phase 3 capability. Every phase 1 GP2 strength read therefore fails with `MissingBasis`, or quietly uses typical values. MFG's bureau coupon plan also fixes no replicate count large enough for a one-sided tolerance limit.
**Evidence:** COV capability rows: "Allowables | Replicates to A- and B-basis values | `InsufficientSamples { needed }` | COV-O-MAT-4 | 3", and "Statistical basis | … | 1".
**Fix:** Move the normal and Weibull one-sided tolerance limits of CMH-17 and MMPDS to phase 1. These are closed-form k-factors on a coupon set, and COV-O-MAT-4's worked examples serve as the oracle. Keep the non-parametric and ANOVA (pooled-batch) methods in phase 3. MFG's as-built strut calibration then states a replicate count per diameter and angle large enough for a B-basis value (the CMH-17 minimum).

### R13-14 · Major · Targets: FLX, COV, MFG
**Section:** FLX "Verification, validity and agreement" (D-FLX-34), "Per-region models" (D-FLX-45); MFG "Molds and injection molding" (D-MFG-15)
**Problem:** D-FLX-34 refines the production mesh three times by r ≥ 1.3. Roache's procedure goes the other way: it coarsens from the production grid, so the cost of solution verification stays below that of the production solve. Refining upward makes `SignOff` impossible on large models. GP5's 3D fill already needs about 5 × 10⁷ cells (MFG's own estimate), and three upward levels of a transient 3D solve multiply that cost about 2.9 times per level, to roughly 25 times. The octree also refines only by r = 2, so r = 1.3 needs new, non-nested octrees, which the text never states. And D-MFG-15 promises "a warpage estimator" that no FLX row defines for the fill–pack–cool–warp chain.
**Evidence:** D-FLX-34: "It refines the final mesh uniformly three times by r ≥ 1.3". NASA Glenn's GCI tutorial (opened) builds the coarser grids by "removing every other grid line", recommends r ≥ 1.1, and uses Fs = 1.25 for three grids.
**Fix:** Change D-FLX-34 so the production mesh is the finest of three grids, with the two coarser ones built by r = 2 octree coarsening, or by a re-rooted octree when r is not an integer. Use p-coarsening for high-order solves. Replace "a warpage estimator" in D-MFG-15 with "calibrated GCI on warpage at measured points (FLX-O-ERR-8)", and cost it in the plan.

### R13-22 · Major · Targets: FLX, MFG, COV
**Section:** FLX "Shells, beams and lattices" (D-FLX-44); MFG as-built strut calibration; COV lattice row
**Problem:** Joint-zone length and stiffness are fitted "against full-resolution unit cells", meaning nominal CAD cells. In laser powder bed fusion, nodes accumulate material and adhered powder, and down-skin struts are oversized, so as-built node stiffness and node stress concentration differ systematically from the nominal geometry. MFG's calibration supplies strut diameter and waviness but not node geometry. Yet GP2's localized strut stress, where cracks start, sits at the nodes.
**Evidence:** D-FLX-44: "Joint-zone length and stiffness are fitted per cell topology against full-resolution unit cells over a relative-density range". MFG lists "diameter and scatter, waviness … and strut-specimen data".
**Fix:** Add CT scans of as-built unit cells from the bureau's phase 1 coupons to MFG's calibration, and fit the node corrections on the CT geometry, with full-resolution immersed solves on the voxel Bodies (finite cell). Fix the oracle split so CT cells from one build plate are held out.

### R15-03 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, COV, MFG
**Section:** Contract 2 (External input, Evidence record); KLN D-KLN-35 and "Physical tests and Evidence records"
**Problem:** Two mechanisms bring signed outside data into a deterministic Evaluation. External inputs are pinned in `ingot.lock` and change "only through an explicit update"; Evidence records are selected by a separate cursor, the evidence watermark, advanced by an audited ref transaction. The glossary even lists "a machine reading" as an External input, which is what an Evidence record is. The watermark adds an input cell, a reporting field, a ref-transaction kind, a "newest record valid at measurement time" lookup and an oracle, and each duplicates what pinning already gives.
**Evidence:** Contract 2, External input: "Signed, content-addressed data produced outside evaluation (… a catalog or regulation dataset, a machine reading). Pinned in `ingot.lock`". Evidence record: "An Evaluation reads the newest record valid at its measurement time … up to the Evaluation's evidence watermark". Releases already "pin the evidence ids they cite".
**Fix:** Make an Evidence record a kind of External input. A rig, lab or gateway result lands in the store; a bot Proposal (or, on protected branches, the same audited transaction that now moves the watermark) pins its id in `ingot.lock` under `[[evidence]]` keyed by `CheckId`, and the check reads the pinned record. Selection ("newest valid at measurement time") moves into the update tool, where it belongs. Delete the watermark from contract 2, KLN's input cells, CI reports and KLN-O-HW-1's watermark clause. One Change then fixes every outcome because its tree pins everything it reads.

### R15-05 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, KLN, AGT, MFG
**Section:** Contract 2 (Waiver, Act), 7.6; KLN "Change orders" (Deviation), "Traceability and compliance" (exemptions); AGT "Acts and approvals"
**Problem:** Four records accept a departure under signature: a Waiver (a `CheckId`, Items, effectivity, reason, expiry), a Deviation ("a temporary departure for given serials or lots, with expiry"), a compliance exemption "signed by `quality`", and a policy allowance. The first three have the same shape. Deviations are granted by `grant_deviation`, an Act, although an Act is defined as "an effect outside Crucible" and AGT files it under the `Record` family with `release` and `yank_package`. Compliance also keeps its own verdict enum (`Compliant`, `NonCompliant`, `Unknown`) beside `CheckOutcome`.
**Evidence:** Contract 2 Waiver: "naming a `CheckId`, the Items and effectivity it covers, a reason and an expiry (a revision, a serial range or a date…)". KLN: "Deviation | A temporary departure for given serials or lots, with expiry, granted by the `grant_deviation` Act"; "exemptions signed by `quality`, and verdicts per revision (`Compliant`, `NonCompliant`, `Unknown`)". AGT: "`grant_deviation` | Record | A deviation against a release".
**Fix:** One Waiver record. A deviation is a Waiver whose effectivity is a serial or lot range against a released revision (MFG's conformance checks are what it waives); a compliance exemption is a Waiver of a compliance check by a principal with the `quality` role. Compliance checks return `CheckOutcome` (`Undecided { MissingData }` for "Unknown"). Redefine Act as "an API method whose effect needs a human Approval of an exact plan", which matches the `Record` family, and drop `grant_deviation` as an Act class.

### R16-01 · Critical · Targets: CONTRACT, AGT, UIX, MFG, KLN
**Section:** Contract 7.6 "Act approvals"; AGT "Approval limits" row; UIX approval page; MFG "Approval, transfer and physical safety"
**Problem:** Every denial blocks re-preparing a plan to the same recipients for 24 hours, and a session may hold at most three pending requests. The rule applies to human-prepared plans as well as agent ones. Approvers use rejection as their normal way to say "fix this": a shop lead rejects a program because of a feed in op 3, and a change board rejects a release because a drawing note is wrong. Under this rule the corrected program cannot go to that machine until tomorrow. A rejected release also blocks every release delivery to the organization's own ERP recipient for a day.
**Evidence:** Contract 7.6: "At most three pending requests per session; a denial blocks re-preparing a plan to the same recipients for 24 h and offers 'report as suspicious', which freezes the session." AGT's capability table: "Three pending; 24 h block after denial … `act/denied_cooldown`". UIX's Proposal review offers "Request changes" (10-ui line 498), but the approval page has only approve and deny. MFG step 4 sends every `Prototype` program through an approved `send_to_machine` plan, so prove-out rejections are routine.
**Fix:** Give the approval page three outcomes. **Approve.** **Return for changes:** closes the request with a reason and no cooldown. A new plan with a different digest may be prepared at once, and resubmitting the same digest is refused with `act/returned_unchanged`. **Deny as suspicious:** today's behaviour, which freezes the session and applies the 24 h block. Count the pending limit per (session, Act class, recipient) and apply it only to contexts with an agent in the delegation chain. Human sessions batch effects into one plan instead. Contract wording: "A return for changes closes the request without penalty; only a denial marked suspicious blocks re-preparing a plan to the same recipients for 24 h and freezes the session." Add AGT-O-9 cases for both outcomes.

### R16-02 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, MFG, KLN
**Section:** Contract 7.6 "Manufacturing programs"; MFG D-MFG-4; KLN D-KLN-38
**Problem:** A program's machine class includes the controller's software version and the post version. Updating the control's software, which shops do for bug fixes and options, therefore moves every approved program out of its class. Released programs then stop flowing through the pull path until each is approved again. A Crucible post-engine release has the same effect: it changes every subject even when re-posting produces identical canonical NC text.
**Evidence:** Contract 7.6: "bound to a machine class (machine package, controller family and software version, post version)". D-MFG-4 repeats it. MFG's canonical hash already covers the posted token stream (D-MFG-24), so the post version adds nothing to what the approver reviewed.
**Fix:** Define the machine class as {machine package digest, controller family, `MotionSemantics` digest, `FileRules` digest}. The controller software version becomes instance data checked at transfer against the package's qualified version range. A version outside the range blocks transfer with `ControllerVersionUnqualified` until MFG-O-POST-3's capture runs once for the new version. If the recaptured `MotionSemantics` digest is unchanged, the class and every approval persist. Remove the post version from the subject. A post bump that reproduces the canonical hash keeps the approval. One that changes it needs a new approval, and the page shows the semantic NC diff.

### R16-04 · Major · Targets: CONTRACT, ELX, MFG, AGT
**Section:** Contract 7.6 "Firmware" and "Approval credentials"; ELX D-ELX-29; MFG "Printers, 3MF and print transfer" and "Approval, transfer and physical safety"

### R16-05 · Major · Targets: MFG, KLN
**Section:** MFG "Approval, transfer and physical safety" (edit capture); MFG "Quality records"; KLN D-KLN-38
**Problem:** Every edit captured at the control opens a Proposal, and parts made from the edited program get a nonconformance record, whatever the program's state. In `Prototype`, editing at the control is the prove-out itself: machinists adjust feeds, depths and clearances on the first parts. The rule therefore turns ordinary prove-out into a stream of nonconformances that a quality system must disposition. It also leaves the first-article record pointing at a program revision that never ran unedited.
**Evidence:** MFG: "An edited program opens a Proposal with its semantic NC diff. Parts made from it get a nonconformance record for disposition (R10-32)." The Nonconformance row covers "parts from programs edited at the control". D-KLN-38 moves a program to `Proven` "after a first-article record".
**Fix:** Split the rule by state. In `Prototype`, a captured edit opens a Proposal on the program Item's branch and enters a prove-out log, and the parts get no nonconformance. The first-article record names the captured revision that actually ran, and `Proven` requires that revision merged into the program source, so the released program is the one that ran. A nonconformance applies only when a `Proven` or `Released` program is edited at the control. Add both cases to MFG-O-SAFE-4.

### R16-06 · Major · Targets: MFG, KLN, AGT

### R16-07 · Major · Targets: MFG, UIX
**Section:** MFG "Milling, drilling and probing strategies"; "Programs, regeneration and agents"; D-MFG-22; UIX toolpath playback
**Problem:** CAM programmers in Mastercam, hyperMILL and NX edit toolpaths every day. They trim passes to a boundary, delete or reorder passes, move an entry point, relink, and slow the feed in one region. MFG and UIX offer strategies, regeneration, playback and an NC panel, but no toolpath edit. Users would have to tune strategy parameters until the path looks right, or hand-edit the NC. A hand-edited program becomes a legacy program that loses associativity. D-MFG-22 also addresses moves by (edge `EntityId`, vertex index), a positional key that regeneration renumbers, so an edit could not survive a design change.
**Evidence:** MFG's capability tables and UIX lines 602 to 634 contain no edit operation on a `Toolpath`. D-MFG-22: "A move is addressed by (edge `EntityId`, vertex index), not by a persistent name."
**Fix:** Strategies emit keyed passes, as pattern generators do: a key of (depth level, region key, pass index along the region's medial order). Add `std::mfg::edit` features written after the strategy call:
- `trim(tp, by: Region | Plane | Boundary, keep:)`
- `remove_passes(tp, keys)`
- `reorder(tp, keys)`
- `relink(tp, policy)`
- `entry(tp, at: Query)`
- `feed_scale(tp, region, factor)`

Each edit resolves by pass key. A key lost after regeneration is a `ResolveError` with fixes, never a silent re-target. Gouge, collision and engagement checks re-run on the edited path. Phase 2, with oracle MFG-O-PROG-6: edits survive a dimension change that keeps the passes, and fail typed when a pass disappears.
